# Dead Letter: story bible

**Status:** the director's direction, 21 October 2026. This governs `docs/scene_overview.md`, `docs/roadmap.md` and
every chapter plan. Where an older document disagrees, this wins; flag the conflict.

## The arc in one paragraph

The refuge was sealed with far more people than it could hold. It survived by **order**: a benevolent, harsh ruler,
the **Steward**, and a council that graded every life and rationed air, food and space by grade, under one motto,
**ORDER ABOVE ALL**.
- **The collapse:** births began to fail. When the systems failed too, order did what it was built to do: it saved the
  top and let the bottom go. The lower rings died in heaps.
- **The Commons:** above, fewer and fewer people were left, and as they thinned the society softened. Grades were
  painted over with names, kitchens were shared, rotas were signed by everyone, and the dead were laid out with care.
- **The protocols:** with almost no children left, the refuge turned all its machinery to protecting the last of them.
- **The breach:** those children grew up over-protected, and as teenagers they rebelled. In one act of rebellion they
  opened a sealed section and let the poisoned air in. It killed the people in that ward, among them the last man.
- **The truth:** Mara, the youngest of them, is the last person who will ever be born in the bunkers.

Humanity withers, one empty ring at a time. Mara keeps working anyway, so that life can spring up again outside.

## The vertical story: a gradient the player walks up

The deeper the ring, the earlier the era, and the more dead there are. The player climbs through history.

| Depth | Era | The dead | The signs | The voices |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The lower works (prologue) | Order and the Failure | Heaps: rows, queues, crowds at doors. | ORDER ABOVE ALL stencilled and enamelled. The Steward's portrait in institutional frames. Grade signs, numbered everything. | Tannoys: announcements that end "Order above all." Cards from the ruled, polite and frightened. |
| Ring B, the school (chapter 1) | Late order, and the protocols beginning | None: the school was left, not died in. | The motto, half painted over. Headcounts falling on chalkboards. New, anxious child-safety notices over old rules. | The register's voice; the lesson; a teacher's unsent cards. |
| The homes (chapter 2) | The Commons | Few, and laid out with care: covered, named. | Grade numbers painted over with family names. Shared-kitchen rotas. A Steward portrait turned to the wall. The teenagers' graffiti over the old motto: *ORDER ABOVE ALL* becomes *WHOSE ORDER?* | Commons council minutes on cards: everyone gets a say. The teenagers' recordings: the Den's club, grown up. |
| The works above (chapter 3) | The Commons, and the thinning | Very few: a memorial wall of names that stops. | Work crews' boards with names crossed off in different hands. Mara's drawings turning into engineering. | Work-crew logs; Mara's early recordings as a young technician. |
| The custody wards (chapter 4) | The protocols, and the breach | The breached ward: a handful, at its sealed door. | Protocol signage everywhere. The forced hatch. Then the birth register. | The breach on an intercom recording (no screaming: a door alarm and someone counting). The last man's card. Mara's own recording, made that night. |
| The top (chapters 5 and 6) | Mara alone | One grave she dug, tended. | Her workshop, her seedlings, a single living tree. | Mara in person. |

## How to tell it

The player must not miss the plot, and must never feel lectured.
- **Two or three independent cues for every beat.** If a player misses the voice card, the room and the signage still
  tell it.
- **Show the change, not the state.** The motto painted over. A portrait turned to the wall. A headcount crossed out
  and rewritten smaller. A rota where one hand signs more and more lines. Time is legible in layers.
- **Voices are people, not narrators.** No one in a recording explains the history. They talk about rations, coats,
  birthdays and who's on the rota. The history is what they take for granted.
- **The big truths land on objects, not speeches.** The birth register's last line. The pale rectangle where a portrait
  hung. The ward door with the forced hatch beside it.
- **The motto is a refrain.** The prologue's tannoys end with "Order above all.", and it is everywhere in the lower
  rings. The player hears it so often it stops meaning anything. Then they find it painted over, then defaced by
  teenagers, then, in Mara's workshop, scratched out on an old enamel plate she uses as a tray for seedlings.
- **Mara's tree is the thread of hope.** It goes from crayon at nine (the prologue), to pencil behind the painted sky
  (chapter 1), to graffiti (chapter 2), to engineering drawings (chapter 3), to a real seedling (chapter 6).

## What changes in what's built

**The prologue** (the Order era): mostly there already, in the grades, the Grade Council, rations and relocation by
grade, and heaps of dead. To add:
- **The motto as a refrain:** the depot, ration and relocation tannoys end "Order above all." (done in the script and
  the game, 21 October). ORDER ABOVE ALL stencilled once or twice per scene in the depot. At most two per scene; it is
  institutional furniture, not a message.
- **The Steward's portrait:** over the ration hatch (DL-05) and in Up Only's lobby (DL-10), and on the relocation
  posters. Stern, kindly painted, in a gilt institutional frame.
- **The order still running:** the relocation announcement already does this. Nothing more is needed.

**Chapter 1, Attendance** (late Order and the first protocols):
- the motto half painted over in the cloakroom;
- the headcount chalked smaller and smaller (214 ... 31 ... 9 ... 4 ... 1);
- the protected-pupil notices, padded corners and grab rails;
- the Steward's portrait over the classroom board.

The DL-12 and DL-13 art agents have these as of 21 October. Mara, a silent pupil, is the last name on the register.

**The overview's chapters** keep their play; their story evidence follows this bible:
- Chapter 2 (Someone Home): the Commons, and the teenagers' rebellion.
- Chapter 3 (The Work Above): the Commons at work, and the thinning.
- Chapter 4 (Protective Custody): the protocols, the breach and the birth register. This is the all-hope-is-lost
  moment, near minute 127, where the overview already places the strongest danger.
