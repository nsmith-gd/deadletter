## 1. The chapter's job

**Place in the story.** Chapter 1 ends on possibility: Mara lived past childhood and went to the Works at fourteen.
DL-17's forwarding points to ring A, and the new cable goes up the stair ahead of D-17. The direct Works door at the
stair head is sealed, so the only way on is through the Commons terraces where the survivors made homes. This chapter
is the game's middle-of-the-heart: the B story becomes a person's whole adult life, read through her rooms.

**Theme, as the addendum puts it.** Nothing stays; the time in between is best spent close to someone. This is the
chapter where Mara is close to someone. It shows all three strands:
- **mending what's near:** her repair on the half landing, and the spare pin she left for whoever came next;
- **making room for what comes next:** a room for one, then a bed for two, then a community that gives up its own
  expansion to grow things;
- **playing for no reason:** the marble run, kept score on by Rowan.
Then Rowan dies, the rota thins, and she is alone. Loneliness, not extinction, is the danger the chapter leaves us with.

**Emotional arc: warmth → ingenuity → grief → generosity.**

| Movement | Scenes | What carries it |
|---|---|---|
| Warmth | DL-18, DL-20 | A room that is cramped but hers; two coats, two bowls, a marble run with a score |
| Ingenuity | DL-19, DL-21, DL-22 | Her repair method; a dinner routine built from irrigation parts; a ration track turned to seed trays |
| Grief | DL-23 | Reconnecting the chair she disconnected; the second bowl at the empty place |
| Generosity | DL-24, DL-25 | The community's surplus given to growing things; D-17 stops the routine and leaves the light on |

**What the player believes.**
- **At the start:** Mara survived and became an apprentice. Someone keeps these lines live. Across the shaft, one window
  is lit: perhaps she is home.
- **At the end:** she had a whole adult life: a room of her own, then a home with Rowan, with play, argument and
  dinners. Rowan died, and she carried on alone, changing her tools to work single-handed. The community, knowing it
  had no future of its own, chose to spend its surplus on growing things. The lit window is a keepsake lamp she still
  keeps burning in an empty flat. She isn't home: she is further up, toward the Works, alone.

**Live question in:** where did the apprentice go, and who keeps the new cable? **Live question out:** the last
consignment and the surplus trunk both go up toward the Works. What is it all for, and is she there?

### DL-18 Room for One: `docs/scenes/DL-18_room_for_one.md` (x 347.0–361.0)

- **Beat:** up DL-17's stair onto ring A. At the stair head the Works door is sealed (a fallen beam, a chain, the Works
  pictogram); across the shaft, high up, one window is lit. The gallery leads to Mara's box room: narrow, one of
  everything, its door to the terrace landing set high in the back wall (1.2).
- **Central inference:** the bed and the bench share one space. Fold one away to have the other.
- **Plausible wrong assumption:** DL-14's lesson ("hold one, move the other"), or simply "more furniture out, more
  steps". He leaves the bed down as a first step and pulls the bench out too. The bench swings onto the mattress with
  a soft thump, the quilt bunching, and rests tilted; climbing it slides him off. The bed alone (0.45) leaves the door
  0.75 up. Nothing prohibits it but the visible shared volume.
- **Elements (3):**
  - the **fold bed**, down at the start, top 0.45 (Use at its worn foot rail folds it up or pulls it down);
  - the **fold bench**, folded on the same steel frame above it (Use at its worn strap pulls it down to its stop at
    0.6 if the space is clear);
  - the high **landing door**.
  The common frame must be plainly visible.
- **Solution:** fold the bed up into its recess; pull the bench down; climb bench, then landing.
- **Reset:** everything moves on input and is counterbalanced. A leaf that meets D-17 stops and waits; nothing
  crushes. Any order works, and either leaf goes back.
- **Story link:** choosing a useful configuration instead of adding things is how a young adult makes a cramped life
  fit. Folding the bed reveals the recess wall: she painted it as a sky with a crooked tree, so lying in bed she looked
  at an outside she made herself.
- **Set and props:**

  - work boots resoled with cut tyre rubber;

- **Curiosities:** a wind-up alarm clock with a pipe clamp for a leg, which rings when touched; the sketch.
- **Light and sound:** the gallery's amber pools from maintained lamps and the relay click every 7 s. The room is dark

- **Directed moments:** none. The arrival framing holds the sealed Works door, the gallery and the lit window

- **Checkpoints:** `ring_a` at 347.6; `terrace` on the landing at 355.8.

### DL-19 A Repair That Held: `docs/scenes/DL-19_a_repair_that_held.md` (x 361.0–377.0)

- **Beat:** along the terrace landing to the stair up. Its **half landing**, a steel platform cantilevered on a forked
  **bracket** and a **stop pin**, droops on its bent pin, tilted so it cannot be stood on. A **jack** (wheeled,
  ratchet, crank) is parked by the wall; a **spare pin** sits in a spring clip on the bracket post.
- **Refinement of §7:** the raised jack stands on the only spot from which D-17 can mantle onto the half landing. So
  he cannot climb while the jack holds it, and the pin swap is required, not optional.
- **Central inference:** another support must take the weight before you remove what is failing.
- **Plausible wrong assumption:** the spare pin says "replace me", so he pulls the bent pin. It slides part-way, and
  the half landing drops onto its visible **safety stops** with a bang, steeper; the pin bites and won't move. The
  failure shows that the platform hangs on that pin. Stepping on it teaches the same: it lurches, and D-17 does
  `Teeter` and hops back.
- **Elements (3):** the **jack** (push, crank), the **bent pin** (Use to pull), the **spare pin** (Use to take, Use to
  seat).
- **Solution:**
  1. Push the jack onto the worn support point (a pocket polished by earlier jacking).
  2. Crank. The half landing lifts off the stops and levels; the bent pin goes slack, its creak stops and it rattles.
  3. Pull the bent pin, and seat the spare when the painted alignment marks on bracket and lug meet.
  4. Crank the jack down into its pocket, draw it back, and climb.
- **Reset:** the jack can be re-raised at any time; no pin can be lost.
- **Story link:** this is Mara's old repair: a strap on the bracket, her tag "M. 349" and her two **witness taps**.
  She clipped the spare for whoever came next. Her repair held; only the pin wore out. The bracket's silhouette is the
  one Mara later fits to D-17's knee (DL-43). Protect it.
- **Set and props:** **Barnaby**, Rowan's plant, alive in a pot wired to the bracket's upper strut. A thin drip tube
  with a little float regulator, teed off the landing's service pipe, ticks once a minute. The pot's tag carries the
  plant's name and Rowan's root glyph; we don't yet know who Rowan is.
- **Curiosity:** the drip tube's tick, and the pot (close look).
- **Light and sound:** pools on the jack and the bracket; the strained pin creaking; the ratchet clicking as the jack
  rises.
- **Directed moment:** `witness`, a 1.5 s close insert (48 mm) as the spare seats: strap, tag, the two taps, D-17's
  head tilting.
- **Wear seed:** after the `Teeter`, a 0.3 s knee settle with a small servo whine (new quirk `Settle`). It never
  changes jump distance.
- **After:** a 30 s walk along the upper terrace, the shaft open beside him. The lit window is closer.
- **Checkpoints:** `half_landing` at 362.0; `repaired` on the upper terrace at 371.0.

### DL-23 The Second Place: `docs/scenes/DL-23_the_second_place.md` (x 424.0–436.0)

## 6. The whimsy moment: the marble run (DL-20)

- **The run.** Down the wall beside the connecting doorway runs a marble run Mara and Rowan built. The left lane is
  hers, copper pipe offcuts with neat bends; the right is his, carved wooden roots that wander. It ends in a bell cup.
  Beside it, chalked in Rowan's hand: "ROOTS 41 · PIPES 39". Nobody else kept count.

  he likes. Each rattles down the whole wall in view and rings the bell. The pipe lane is always faster, so the score
  is a joke he can't fix. New quirk `Follow`: his head tracks the marble down; `Proud` plays on the bell.
- **Rules.** It opens nothing, appears in no hint, and any input cancels it. It is authored motion, not physics.
