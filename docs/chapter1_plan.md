# Chapter 1 "Undelivered": the rework

**Status:** approved by the director, 18 October 2026; in production (`docs/chapter1_build_spec.md`)
**Rules:** `docs/puzzle_principles.md`
**Target:**

| | Now | Target |
|---|---|---|

| Floor (no hesitation) | 3:54 | 8 minutes or more |

## 1. What is wrong now

| Scene | Mechanic | Arc step reached | Floor | Simulated first-time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DL-01 The Wake | latch, crank | introduce | 0:31 | 1:13 |
| DL-02 The Weight of the Job | carry; substitution (weight on a plate) | introduce | 0:41 | 1:47 |
| DL-03 Return to Sender | routing (belt, points, carriers) | introduce | 1:04 | 2:39 |
| DL-04 The Handbrake | substitution again; latch (pawl); a timed crossing | introduce (the pawl) | 0:59 | 2:02 |
| DL-05 The Child at the Address | pull | introduce | 0:38 | 1:31 |

**What's wrong:**
- Every scene introduces a mechanic, and none develops one.
- DL-04 repeats DL-02's thought: load to raise something.
- The pawl has no reason to exist beyond "the platform drops if you don't": the player never needs the state to stay
  after taking the weight away, which is what a latch is for.
- There is too little world between puzzles to say what happened down here, and no optional discoveries beyond a
  lamp cord.

**What works and stays:**
- the wake;
- the realisation among the couriers;
- the pit;
- the look back and the hopscotch;
- the city reveal;
- the bedroom.

## 2. The ladder

Chapter 1 runs four mechanic families through their arcs, and the finale combines them.

| Family | Introduce | Develop | Twist | Combine |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Push and climb** | 1: push a crate to the trench and use it as a step | 3: the trolley | 7: the trolley as a step, not a weight | 9: push the duct across the water |
| **Carry** | 2: carry a box and stack it to climb | 3: the weight onto the trolley | 4: carry a fallen courier | 6: load a carrier |
| **Substitution** (something holds a state after he leaves) | 3: weight on the plate holds the gate | 4: the bay wants a courier, not a weight | 6: a routed carrier delivers the weight for him | 7: the pawl holds the platform once the weight is taken away |
| **Routing** | 5: stop, switch, restart, ride | 6: send a loaded carrier where you can't go | (Chapter 2: moving occupants) | 6 |
| **Latch and hold** | 1: release the shutter latch | 7: set the pawl | 8: set a pawl on a slipping cage, against time | 8 |

**Rule of thumb:** no two consecutive puzzles ask for the same thought.

## 3. The nine scenes

Times are first-time estimates, with the floor in brackets. **New** marks a new scene; the others rework a built one.
The numbering is a proposal (see section 6).

### 1. The Wake (DL-01, reworked): about 2:00 (0:45)

As built: the wake, the parcel lamp, the cord lamp, crouch under the shutter, the latch, the climb, the crank.

**New:**
- **Teach push:** the trench is too wide to jump. A small crate stands beside it. Push it in and it becomes a step.
  The crate already there is climbed, as now.
- **Curiosity:** the parcel scale at the dispatch counter. Step on and its needle swings to "postage due": a small
  joke, and a promise that he is a courier.
- **Tableau:** the dispatch board with D-17's route still chalked on it.

### 2. The Sorting Hall (new): about 1:45 (0:40)

The hall beyond the bulkhead, where post came in by pneumatic tube.

**Puzzle (carry, introduce):**
- The way on is a gantry above a long sorting table.
- A parcel box lies on the floor. Carry it to the table's foot, climb onto it, then up to the gantry.
- The first try is to jump from the floor, which nearly works, so the answer is close at hand.

**Curiosities:**
- **The tube:** as he passes, a pneumatic tube coughs out one ancient letter, the first undelivered letter. A red
  mail slot stands across the hall. Posting the letter stamps it RETURN, with a thud. This teaches the chapter's secret
  verb, safely.
- **The courier board:** the tea corner, with named mugs, and the depot's "courier of the year" board, its last
  photograph faded to a silhouette.

**Story:** a pictogram notice, "ring C deliveries suspended", with a water-drop symbol and a date. The first
hint of the flood (see section 4).

### 3. The Weight of the Job (DL-02, kept): about 2:30 (0:40)

As built: the plate, the gate, the weight, the trolley, the racks lighting up. This is substitution, introduce, and
carry, develop.

**Changes:**
- The plate and the gate are linked by a visible cable run along the wall, so cause and effect read without a cut.
- The racks reveal stays: it's the scene's emotional beat.

### 4. The Racks (new): about 2:30 (0:45)

Among the dormant couriers. The realisation plays here, as now.

**Puzzle (substitution, develop and twist; carry, twist):**
- The exit is a charging bay door. Its plate carries a courier pictogram.
- D-17 steps on: the bay reads him, its three lamps light, and the door opens. He steps off: it closes, as in scene 3.
- **Wrong assumption:** fetch the trolley. It can't follow him: it is holding the gate behind him, and taking it off
  traps him.
- **The answer:** one of his own. A courier lies fallen from its rack. D-17 lifts it, slowly and carefully, carries
  it to the bay and sets it down. The bay charges it. Its lamps flicker once, weakly, as if it might wake, and then
  hold steady. The door opens and D-17 walks on, leaving it there.
- **Why:** "the mechanic is the story": ordinary equipment, one of many, and a success that leaves something behind.

**Emotional shot:** none. The realisation, minutes earlier, is this stretch's shot; the flicker plays in the side view.

### 5. Return to Sender (DL-03, kept): about 2:45 (1:05)

As built: the scanner's refusal, the pit, the label check, stop, switch, restart, ride. This is routing, introduce.

**Changes:**
- The route's lamps and the points' blade are lit in the side view, so the ramp reads without a shot.

### 6. The Heap (new): about 2:30 (1:00)

The ride up the ramp now ends short of the gallery.

**Puzzle (routing, develop; substitution, twist; carry, combine):**
- **The block:** the gallery's gate is a counterweighted shutter. Its weigh bucket hangs at the ramp's head, where
  carriers tip their load.
- **Wrong assumption:** ride up with nothing. It doesn't work.
- **The answer:** send a delivery. Carry a sorting weight from the heap's edge into an empty carrier at the dock,
  route it to the ramp, and watch it climb and tip into the bucket. The gate rises. Then ride the next carrier up.
- **Optional:** climb down onto the heap of dead letters, where one envelope is lit by a shaft from above. This is
  the chapter's **major secret letter**: a child's drawing on its back, addressed to ring C.

### 7. The Handbrake (DL-04, reworked): about 3:00 (1:10)

The goods lift, as built, through the pawl. The trolley rides the pan, the platform rises, it teeters under him,
and he sets the pawl.

**New twist (substitution with the latch):**
- The far landing is a step too high for D-17 to climb.
- **The answer:** the pawl means the platform no longer needs the trolley. Pull the trolley off the pan (the
  platform stays up, which is the point of the latch), push it across onto the platform and to the far landing, and
  climb up off it.
- **Wrong assumption:** leave the trolley as it was, which is what every earlier scene taught.
- **Why:** this makes the pawl matter.

Then the returns belt crossing, as built: an execution test, with a checkpoint before it.

### 8. The Slipping Cage (new): about 2:00 (1:00)

The chapter's set piece and finale.

**The set piece (latch, twist under pressure):**
- The belt leads to the residential access shaft and an old freight cage. D-17 rides it up.
- Halfway, its brake gives: it shudders (the "stumble" from the playtest, now on purpose), then slips.
- The cage has its own pawl lever. Throw it as the cage passes a lit landing and it bites. Too early or too late and
  it rattles on the rack and the cage slides back to the bottom.
- **No death:** the checkpoint is the cage floor, and he is back in control at once.

**Then the release:**
- the tunnel, the look back, the hopscotch;
- the balcony and the city reveal, the chapter's one wide shot;
- **background event:** far across the chasm, a cargo lift full of furniture climbs toward the lit upper city, and
  never comes back down.

### 9. The Child at the Address (DL-05, reworked): about 3:30 (1:20)

Down to ring C, as built: the seal, the dark lane, C-114, the knock, the rabbit, the desk, the marks, the terminal.

**New:**
- **Tableau:**
  - A dried tideline runs across every home in ring C at 1.2 m, above D-17's head.
  - Inside C-114, the growth marks run above it. The flood took the lower part of the room; the marks survived
    because they were high.
- **Puzzle (push, combine):**
  - Halfway along the lane, the floor has sunk into standing water. D-17 won't step into it: he flinches and
    sparks, once, safely.
  - The fallen duct is the bridge. Push it across the water, then walk it.
- **Curiosities, beside the path:**
  - C-108: through its window, a table still set for a meal.
  - C-110: letters jammed in its slot. Pull one free and post it in ring C's red slot: the second letter.
  - C-112: the door taped shut with the water-drop pictogram.

**Emotional shots:** the marks and the terminal. The rabbit is a short push-in that never takes control.

## 4. What happened down there (the story spine)

A proposal for the lower levels' history, told only through the world.

| Refuge year | What happened | Where the player reads it |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to about 30 | The refuge is dug downward as the city grows. The lowest levels (the depot, the works, ring C) house the people who keep it running. | The scale of the depot, the courier board, the busy day board in DL-03 (1,240 deliveries a day). |
| about 30 to 41 | Births slow. The city starts to pull upward into homes left empty above. Moving up is the promise. | Painted UP arrows; lift timetables; the cargo lift background event. |
| 42 | Water breaks into ring C. The depot's power to the ring is cut mid-shift. D-17 was carrying a birthday parcel to a six-year-old in C-114, and stops where he stands. | D-17's wake in the dark; the "deliveries suspended" notice; the tideline; the parcel's stamp date. |
| 43 | Ring C is evacuated and sealed. The families go up. The returns belt keeps bringing ring C's post back down. | RING C SEALED · P.A. 311; the queue line to the lift with dropped belongings; the heap of dead letters. |
| 43 to 109 | The lower levels run on by themselves: belts, scanners, charging bays, empty routines. | The scanner's refusal; the bay that charges a dead courier. Chapter 2's school continues this. |
| 118 (now) | Someone above has restored power. It reaches the depot, and D-17 wakes. | The opening. Paid off by the workshop relay later (the overview's continuity note). |

**Why water:**
- It ties ring C's loss to the water work of Chapter 4 and to Mara's later irrigation drawings.
- The thing that took her childhood home becomes the thing she spent her life learning to tend.
- It is visible without words: tidelines, standing water, swollen doors.

## 7. Building it

1. **Engineering first:**
   - Give each scene its own x range, through a per-scene offset in the Blender builders and the game modules, so
     scenes can be inserted without hand-shifting everything after them.
   - Respawn under three seconds, with music continuing.
2. **Build and measure each new scene in turn:**
   - Scenes 01b, 02b, 03b and 04b, then the reworks of 01, 04 and 05.
   - Extend the simulated player (`tools/sim/`) to each scene as it lands. Check its floor and first-time estimate
     against this plan before moving on.
3. **Then** a timed playtest of the whole chapter by someone who hasn't played it.

## 8. Decisions (director, 18 October 2026)

1. **The flood is canon.** Water broke into ring C in P.A. 310; the ring was sealed in P.A. 311.
2. **The fallen courier is the right tone.** D-17 drags one of his own onto the bay, and leaves it there.
3. **Renumber.** The game is now 49 scenes (section 6).
4. **Letters can change the epilogue, but the story must be understood without them.**
   - Many players don't like reading, so visuals are their story language. Every essential story beat is carried by
     an image, an action or a tableau, never by a letter, a sign or a screen alone.
   - Letters add depth for those who look.
5. **Sheepy:** a stopwatch log of one playthrough is still worth doing, to give us real numbers for its pacing.
