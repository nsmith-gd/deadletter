## The problem in one line

DL-12, DL-13 and DL-14 each **introduce** a relationship (two conditions, a cycle, paired outputs). After them, DL-15
to DL-17 introduce more small ideas instead of **combining** the ones just learned, so the difficulty plateaus, and
DL-17 has no inference at all.

## The shape: introduce, combine, reframe

Nine scenes (three new, DL-17 reworked), each with more steps or one more idea than the scene before. New scenes use
lettered IDs, so the plan's stable IDs (DL-12 to DL-51) don't move.

## The new and reworked scenes

### DL-14a Lights Out (new, after the duct, before the sick bay)

**Story.** The dormitory: forty beds, three made up and pushed together. A night-light painted as a sun. REST HOUR
rules on the wall; a nurse's chair facing the beds. You must be kept: even sleep was supervised.

**Goal.** The door to the sick bay wing opens only while the room reads DARK on its lux dial (an eye on the wall).

**Mechanics (all taught already).** The night-light dims and brightens on a cycle, with a dial and a brake (DL-13).
Two blinds on one cord cover the skylight and the corridor's glass door: as one comes down the other goes up (DL-14).

**Play.** Brake the night-light in its dim phase. Wind the cord: the skylight blind comes down, the door blind goes up,
corridor light floods in. The blinds' bottom rails carry coat pegs. Fetch the dressing gown from the nurse's chair,
climb the bunk ladder with it, and hang it on the skylight blind's peg: its weight holds that blind down. Wind again:
the clutch slips, the door blind comes down alone. DARK; the door opens.

**Wrong first try.** Use the pole, as in DL-14. It isn't here; the room has a peg on the blind instead. Or hang the
gown on the wall's pegs, where gowns go.

**The aha.** A peg is for a coat (DL-12); a coat on a peg can also be a weight. It holds the blind the way the pole did.

### DL-14b Unaccompanied (new, the corridor to the sick bay)

**Story.** NO PUPIL UNACCOMPANIED. The escort trolley still runs its round: a slow rail machine with a lamp and a bell.
It escorts any pupil it finds back to the dormitory, gently. The plan's attendant (chapter 4) is a different machine;
this one only follows its rail and its rule.

**Goal.** Reach the sick bay door at the corridor's end while the trolley patrols between.

**Mechanics.** The trolley's rule is visible and stable: its lamp's cone marks what it sees; anything pupil-sized in the
cone gets a bell and an escort back along the rail (it pushes a figure ahead of it; it herds D-17 back to the
dormitory door, unhurt). A teaching figure waits in a side bay on a short spur.

**Play.** Watch one round. Push the figure out of its bay onto the main rail when the trolley turns at the far end. It
sees the figure, rings, and escorts it all the way back to the dormitory, which takes it the length of the corridor and
round the bend. Walk on behind it while it's busy. A side niche halfway lets a careful player wait out a second pass.

**Wrong first try.** Run for it. The cone catches him and he is walked back (no death, a few seconds).

**The aha.** The figure has been a weight, a key and a companion; here it's a decoy. The machine wants a pupil, any
pupil.

### DL-16a Her Way Round (new, after the rack, before the workshop)

**Story.** The rev 0.3 plan's "Break into Two": D-17 follows the child's workaround. The way to the workshop is barred
(PUPILS NOT PERMITTED BEYOND THIS POINT). Low on the wall, at child height, a crooked tree in chalk, then another, and an
arrow: the kitchen's dumbwaiter. She went round.

**Mechanics (from the prologue).** A dumbwaiter car on a counterweight, sized for meal trays (DL-07, DL-09, DL-10), with
a load gauge (DL-09's new face). A call bell sends the car to the other floor.

**Play.** The car is too light for its counterweight with him in it: ring the bell and it shoots up, the safety catch
bites, and it lowers him back (no death). Fill the car with ballast to the gauge's line: books from the kitchen's
shelf, a ration tin, carried one at a time. One book too many and it won't rise at all. Stand in it, ring, ride up.
At the top a tray hatch: and a fourth chalk tree, and a small handprint.

**Wrong first try.** Ride up as he is; or overload it.

**The aha.** As in DL-10, he is the load; this time the counterweight is the thing to match, and the gauge says by how
much.

### DL-17 A Different Lesson (reworked into a puzzle)

**Story.** Her drawing, read as instructions. Lisle's test rig on the bench: a small tank, three sluices A, B, C, a bed
with a float. "Apprentice test: water bed 1." The stair door to ring A is on the same test's latch: the float lifts it.

**Play.** Lift the tray off the drawer, read the drawing and the card (as now). Open the tank: the water goes everywhere
and the float barely moves. Her note 2 says "sluices A B C: shut two, water the third"; her bed is bed 1. Shut A and
C, open B: bed 1 fills, the float rises, the latch lifts, the stair door swings open. The stamp, and he leaves.

**Wrong first try.** Open everything; or water the wrong bed.

**The aha.** The record isn't only a story: it is how to do it. Her drawing still works.

**A flag.** Water as weight and float is the plan's chapter 3 introduction (DL-26). This is a small, safe preview at
her test rig. If that should stay new for DL-26, the rig can work with sand or ball bearings instead.

## Production

- **Layout.** The school is one continuous strip of x. Inserting DL-14a and DL-14b means moving DL-15 to DL-17 (art and
  game constants) about 30 m to the right; DL-16a adds about 14 m before DL-17. I'd give the school's later scenes an
  offset constant, as the prologue's OFF2 to OFF5 work, rather than move every number by hand.
- **Art.** Three new set modules (dl14a, dl14b, dl16a) by art agents; DL-17's rig in dl17.
- **Game.** The night-light cycle, the peg weight, the escort trolley (its rule, its cone, its herding), the dumbwaiter
  with ballast and gauge, the sluices and float.
- **Length.** The director's run was 12 minutes. Three new scenes at 3 to 5 minutes each and a reworked DL-17 should
  take a first-time player to about 25 to 30 minutes.
