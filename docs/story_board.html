    Story bible notes

    How to tell it

    • Action, consequence, corroboration. Every essential inference comes from something the player does, a visible result, and other evidence agreeing. Optional words deepen; they never carry the only proof.
    • Show the change, not the state. The motto painted over. A second chair folded away. A rota where one hand signs more and more lines. Labels growing larger and shakier.
    • Voices are people, not narrators. They talk about coats, bowls, rotas and fittings. The history is what they take for granted.
    • Show what people did, not how they died. No cause-of-death signs, no corpse reveal once the dead become few.
    • No villains, no saints. Order could be privately kind; the Commons stubborn and inefficient; the attendant constrains and later catches. No patient loses air to grow a tree.
    • Don't end every shot on the green flag. Alternate inference, short confident application and quiet consequence. Music need not swell at every reveal; let wind survive the finale's score.

    Threads that run through it

    • The crooked tree: a child's imagination, then a workable engineering approximation, then Rowan's reminder about roots, then irregular growth of its own. Not a quest arrow.
    • Two places: desks, dinner, the withdrawn chair, the shared jig adapted for one, the joint repair, the final seat.
    • Hands: the tow loop, the empty place, the worn handrail, the cradle, working human hands, mutual support, bark.
    • Water: the feared sump, rationed supply, stored surplus, roots, an ordinary stream.
    • The motto: enamel authority, a partial paint-over, names and practical repairs, the old sign reused face down as a work surface. Never an increasingly explicit slogan.
    • Light: the assignment lamp, the painted sun, the keepsake lamp, nursery daylight, the occupied workshop, open air.
    • Two witness taps: Mara's repairs carry two small taps; when she repairs D-17 she makes the same two taps with her spanner.

    Who's who

    • D-17, a courier with one parcel, seventy-six years late. Wants to deliver it; understands help as correct execution; needs to learn that care includes attention, receiving help, and staying after the job ends. Precise and earnest from the start. No hidden human soul.
    • Mara, born P.A. 302, about 85 now, an irrigation technician and the last human alive. Practical: a feel for pressure, an ugly repair that works, no patience for inaccessible bolts. Amused, sometimes curt, quietly playful. Wants to finish her bypass; has always wanted to touch a real tree.
    • Her parent, who sent the boots: hopeful for one child's ordinary future, not a prophet.
    • Lisle, the school volunteer. Helped her test an idea; his corrections sit beside her drawing. She asked for the placement.
    • Abel and Orme, her friends. One adds extravagant creatures to drawings; the other corrects impossible mechanisms, then adds jokes. Their marks grow into adult work.
    • Rowan (proposed), Mara's partner, a propagation worker who insists roots won't obey her tidy diagrams. A chipped bowl, a coat, handwriting, failed experiments. Dies before the game.
    • The attendant, a protective machine with stable rules. It constrains and supports; never a villain or a philosopher.
    • The Steward and the Grade Council, the Order era: graded every life. Portraits in gilt frames.
