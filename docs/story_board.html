Story bible notes

How to tell it

  • Two or three cues for every beat. If a player misses the voice card, the room and the signage still tell it.
  • Show the change, not the state. The motto painted over. A portrait turned to the wall. A headcount crossed out and written smaller.
  • Voices are people, not narrators. They talk about rations, coats, birthdays and rotas. The history is what they take for granted.
  • Big truths land on objects. The register's last line. The pale rectangle where a portrait hung.
  • Show what people did, not how they died. Tableaux of natural reactions; no cause-of-death signs.

Threads that run through it

  • The motto, ORDER ABOVE ALL: everywhere in the lower rings, then painted over, then defaced by teenagers, then scratched out on a plate Mara uses as a seedling tray.
  • Mara's crooked tree: crayon at nine (prologue), pencil behind the painted sky (chapter 1), graffiti (chapter 2), engineering drawings (chapter 3), a real seedling (the end).
  • The vertical gradient: the deeper the ring, the earlier the era and the more dead. The player climbs through history.
  • The lights came on: someone turned them on. Every chapter brings D-17 closer to her.

Who's who

  • D-17, a courier robot with one parcel: a birthday gift for Mara, nine, from a parent on the works. Seventy-six years late.
  • Mara D., born P.A. 302, the last birth anyone records. A silent pupil who drew trees. Old now, still working above.
  • The Steward and the Grade Council: benevolent, harsh, graded every life. Portraits in gilt frames.
  • The Den: the last children's club. Password: crooked tree.
