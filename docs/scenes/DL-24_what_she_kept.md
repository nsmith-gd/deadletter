# DL-24 What She Kept: production plan

**Status:** design for director review, 26 October 2026 (task C-01, #6). Nothing here is built.
**Contract:** `docs/chapter2_plan.md` §4 (DL-24 brief and "Fixed across scenes"), which wins over this doc.
**Sources:** `docs/full_game_plan.md` §7 DL-24, §2's moral complication, §8, and the 23 October addenda.
**Sequence:** DL-23 → DL-24 → DL-25, with no cut on either side.
**Lane:** x 436.0–448.0, floor 6.4 throughout. Heights below are above that floor unless they say "datum".
**Gear:** look. **First play:** 2.0 minutes. **Difficulty:** 1. **Tension:** low, reflective.

## 1. What the scene must do

| Requirement | How the scene meets it |
|---|---|
| A quiet gear between grief (DL-23) and the finale (DL-25) | One action, no wrong answer, no music cue. The longest silence in the chapter. |
| Fold the workbench to clear the exit | Mara's bench is folded down across the balcony, top 0.8 (too high to mantle), its end against the exit door at 444.0. Use at its worn fold handle and it swings up against the back wall on its counterweight. |
| She kept the work and changed the tools | Folded up, the bench shows its underside: a long two-worker pipe-bending jig. The far station has a foot strap, a sprung catch and a clamp where a second pair of hands went. Working alone, not injury: every change replaces *another* person's hands, never her own. |
| The community's changed purpose, without words | Through the door, the outer gallery looks over the common hall. The allocation board shows the terraces in section, the upper homes overpainted with seed trays and arrows up to the Works. |
| A collective decision, argued over years | Different hands on the board, dates struck out and repainted, the council rule across its head. |
| The cancelled terrace is real | Beyond the board, half-built house shells stacked with nursery pipe, with the same silhouette as the board's overpainted rows. |
| Generosity involved compromise, not giving up every pleasure | The party room beside the board (the smaller common room the revised plan built): every cup washed and upturned. The optional debate shows the argument and the compromise. |
| Other shelters fell silent | Optional: the network slips on their spike, thinning to nothing. |
| The essential look is under 30 s, in the normal camera | The framing widens at the door; nothing is shown only in an authored shot. |

## 4. The puzzle

**None, by design** (§7 and the chapter plan's §8: bends principles 4 and 7). Folding furniture is now confident
vocabulary (DL-18, DL-20), so the bench is a short application with one element and no wrong answer. The scene's work
is looking. The only "wrong" try, climbing a 0.8 top, is corrected in under a second by the slide back and the lit
handle at his feet. **Reset:** none needed; the leaf stays up.

## 5. New models

- **The work corner:**
  - A shadow board: tools hung on painted outlines, edged with small painted leaves in Rowan's hand. One outline
    is empty, a short spanner she took with her. It teaches "a painted silhouette is where a thing lives" one scene
    before DL-25's park outline.
  - A vice with jaws faced in copper; a pipe rack, offcuts graded by diameter; a stool with one leg sleeved in pipe.
  - The bench lamp on a jointed arm, angled at the bench top.
- **The workbench:**
  - The top, scarred and burnt, with a front apron and its worn fold handle. Two strap hinges, the counterweight box,
    the chain and pulley, and the wall hook that latches it up.
  - **The jig** on the underside:
    - a curved steel bending former 1.6 m long and a roller carriage on a track;
    - two handle stations, a long lever at each end. The near station (left) has its paired grips worn bright.
    - The far station (right) has its lever grips dull under dust, and three later additions worn bright:
      - a stirrup with a foot strap cut from tyre rubber (as on her boots in DL-18);
      - a sprung catch: a toothed quadrant and dog that holds a bend's angle while she moves;
      - a screw clamp bolted where the second worker held the pipe's end.
    - Rowan's three-stroke root glyph is scratched by the far lever. The clamp's base plate is notched so it doesn't
      cover it.
- **Lisle's bench sign:** "BREAK IT HERE, NOT OUT THERE", as §5.
- **The outer gallery:** the door, a rail, a small desk with the spike of network slips, a noticeboard.
- **The common hall** (far detail: flat quads, no worn-paint shader, no shadows):
  - **The allocation board.** The terraces in section, stencilled long ago in black and white as rows of pitched-roof
    house blocks with grade numbers. Over the top two rows, painted over the houses:
    - green seed-tray blocks with sprout ticks;
    - fat yellow chevron arrows climbing to the Works pictogram at the top (the one on DL-18's sealed door);
    - the grade numbers painted out and family names written in, in many hands;
    - date tags beside each change, struck out and repainted ("P.A. 341" → "344" → "349");
    - one block with a big room's outline ghosted under a smaller room hung with bunting, beside a small tray block
      (the compromise, matching the debate);
    - one neat caret correction;
    - across the head, the council rule: "ARGUE WITH THE PLAN, NOT THE PERSON".
  - **The shell terrace.** Four half-built house shells, no roofs, with the same stepped roofline as the board's
    overpainted row. Pipe bundles through the door holes, tray stacks to the sills, a hand hoist, and the green tray
    stencil on each wall.
  - **The party room.** A glazed front onto the hall. Inside, a long trestle table under a white cloth, every cup
    washed and upturned in rows, each cup different. Chairs stacked on the other tables, faded bunting, and the banner
    "LAST ONES OUT DO THE WASHING UP".

## 8. Music and sound

- **No cue.** DL-23's `second_place` decays on the balcony; DL-24 adds nothing (§9: music need not swell at every
  reveal). This is the quietest scene in the chapter.
- **The work corner:** close air, the bench lamp's faint tick, the counterweight chain's run and the hook's clack.
- **The hall:** a long reverb on every footstep, slow air movement, bunting stirring, one far drip. The absent crowd
  is the sound: no voices, no machines.
- **The arm:** every 12 s, a rattle along a rail and a small *tink*, through the kitchen wall. It grows from 443 to 448
  and leads him on.

## 9. Checkpoints

- None new. `balcony` (434.0, DL-23) covers the scene: at most 14 m of easy walking to repeat.

## 10. Acceptance checks

- **Goal in frame:** from 438.0, the fold handle's light pool and the door frame are in one 720p frame.
- **The climb:** a first-time player who tries to climb the bench sees the claw-and-slide-back within 1.0 s, with the
  lit handle within 0.6 m of D-17.
- **The fold:** Use to latch takes 1.6 s, and the doorway is passable 0.2 s after.
- **The jig:** it is fully lit and in frame for at least 4 s after the fold at run speed.
- **The board, wordless:** shown a 720p still of `hall wide`, at least 4 of 5 people who have not played describe
  homes painted over with growing things, in any words.
- **The look:** at run speed, the board, the shells and the party room are all in frame together for at least 2.5 s.
  A player who stops gets no prompt.
- **A clean play plane:** nothing in the hall has wear or a light pool; in playtests, no one tries to reach the hall.
- **Time:** mechanical floor about 11 s (12 m at 1.6 m/s, plus the fold). First play target 2.0 minutes, with the
  essential look under 30 s and curiosities optional.
- **Quiet:** no music cue starts in DL-24.
