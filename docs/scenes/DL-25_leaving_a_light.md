## 4. The puzzle

**Builds on:** the coupling (DL-23), one route at a time (DL-22), reading a cycle (DL-21), a catch that takes what
passes (DL-22), support transfer (DL-19), the slipping clutch (DL-14).
**Central inference:** stop the routine safely before you take its power. Park it, then unplug.

**Elements (4):** the serving arm (cycle), the park catch, the arm's coupling, the lift's coupling.

**The cycle** (12 s, from leaving park): 0.0–0.4 lifts out of its outline; 0.4–3.5 runs down the rail; 3.5 *clack*
into the reach notch; 3.5–4.1 swings across the lift door; 4.1–6.2 lifts the dish lid, holds it, sets it back
(*tink*); 6.2–6.8 folds up; 6.8–10.1 is driven back up the rail; 10.1–10.5 folds into its outline, *clack*;
10.5–12.0 pauses at park. The lift doorway is shut only from 3.5 to 6.8 while the arm is powered.

**Plausible wrong assumptions, and how the world corrects each:**
- **"Unplug the arm, plug the lift."** The obvious read of one socket and two plugs (DL-23's lesson).
  - The hum stops, its work lamp and the hatch glow go out, and the trolley runs down the rail, its dog scraping
    louder, into the reach notch: *clack*. The forearm swings across the doorway. At most 3.6 s from any phase; on the
    return trip the trolley visibly stops and rolls back down.
  - If he then plugs the lift, its pilot turns green and its bars rise behind the arm. Walking in, he stops against
    the forearm and puts a claw on it; it doesn't move.
  - **Correction:** the arm is in the way, and it must go home before it loses power. Re-plug it and it resumes at once.
- **"Use the lamp's power."** The lit lamp and its fresh tape draw the eye.
  - Use at the splice box: `Tug`. He pinches the thin plug, pulls gently, the tape holds, and he smooths it down. Carrying
    a coupling there, he offers its brass plug: twice the size, and no collar takes it. Corrected within 2 s.
- **"Set the catch, then unplug straight away."** Unplugging before the arm has come home is the first wrong case. The
  arm stops across the door while the set catch waits, empty, under the outline: the picture says "wait for it".

**Solution:**
1. Set the park catch (Use at its lever under the outline). On its next return the arm folds in and its cradle eye drops
   onto the hook. At 0.0 the drive tries to lift it out, the hook holds, and the clutch slips and whirrs. Through the
   clutch housing's window, a chalk line on the plate spins.
2. Unplug the arm's coupling. The whirr winds down, and the dog drops into the park notch.
3. Take the lift's coupling from its hook and plug it into the socket. The pilot turns green and the bars rise. Ride.

**Also valid:** unplugging while the arm is folded or folding at park (10.1 to 0.4): the dog is in the park notch.
**Reset:** none needed. Re-plugging the arm always restores motion; Use lifts a set catch. Nothing is permanent until he rides.

**Against `docs/puzzle_principles.md`:** meets all. Rule 2: the combine of route, catch and cycle. Rule 4: DL-23's
"plug it" applied too broadly, answered in one frame. Rule 6: the chain, rail and leads link every cause to its effect.

## 11. Acceptance checks

- **Goal in frame:** from 448.6, at 720p, the frame shows the moving arm, the outline and catch, the socket and its
  plug, the dark pilot and closed bars, the coiled coupling, and the lamp.
- **Wrong 1:** a first-time player who unplugs the arm at any phase except park sees it reach the far notch, across
  the doorway, within 3.6 s. The work lamp and the hatch glow go out within 0.3 s of the unplug.
- **Wrong 1, then the lift:** with the arm across, plugging the lift turns the pilot green and raises the bars within
  1.0 s; walking in is stopped at the forearm in 100% of tries. Re-plugging the arm restarts it within 0.5 s.
- **The catch:** set while the arm is away, the arm hooks on its next return (at most 12 s), and the whirr starts within
  0.3 s of the drive trying to lift it.
- **The solution:** unplugging while caught, or between 10.1 and 0.4, leaves the arm in its outline; the lift then boards.
- **The lamp:** no sequence of socket, catch and arm phase puts it out or dims it (an exhaustive state test; the arm's
  passing shadow aside). `Tug` starts within 0.3 s of Use at the splice box.
- **No dead ends:** every state is recoverable without the checkpoint, by re-plugging the arm, within 12 s.
- **Readability:** in 720p stills, 4 of 5 testers call the park outline "where the arm goes" and the forearm "in the way".
- **Time:** mechanical floor about 30 s from 448.6 to boarding (set the catch, walk to the socket while the arm returns,
  unplug, fetch and plug the lift's coupling, board). First play target 4.0 minutes, including `light`: a median of at
  most two wrong tries, and no tester over 6 minutes.
- **`light`:** each peak lands no more than 45 ms early or 125 ms late on its authored frame; control returns at 26 s.
