# Dead Letter — Reworked story, world, and puzzle plan

**Revision 0.3 · 21 October 2026 · Director workshop proposal**

This replaces the previous full-game plan. Mechanical foundation: `dead-letter-gameplay.md`, DL-01–51. Preserve the eleven prologue puzzles. Retain stable IDs and the current school order. Later changes below are proposals, not verified implementation. The source GDD and storyboards remain unedited.

## 1. The story

A little courier wakes beneath the last human refuge with a birthday parcel seventy-six years overdue. It follows a girl through the places where she learned, loved, worked, and grew old. Along the way, the player discovers that the remnants of humanity, unable to secure a future for themselves, spent their remaining lives repairing the living world above them.

The courier finds its recipient: Mara, an elderly irrigation technician still tending that inheritance. Helping each other, they complete one modest part of her work and step outside. The parcel contains the little outdoor boots her parent hoped she would someday need. She cannot wear them. She can finally stand where they were meant to take her.

She sits beneath a crooked tree. The delivery completes. The robot sits beside her because it wants to remain.

**Primary theme: a future is worth caring for even when it will not belong to us.**

Its intimate expression is that another person's life matters beyond a task or a useful function. Humanity makes a place for other life; a parent makes a gift for a child's imagined future; Mara makes a place beside her for a machine whose job is done.

Humanity's disappearance is a tragedy. The game does not celebrate extinction as a deserved punishment or offer ecological recovery as absolution. Nor does every last resident become a saint. What moves us is a community gradually choosing care without the promise that their descendants will receive its reward.

### What the player wants, in order

Complete a delivery → discover whether the child survived → understand the life she built → understand why people kept the Works running → reach the person above → accept help when D17 cannot continue alone → help Mara experience the world she helped restore → stay after the job is finished.

The player is never required to explain the entire fictional catastrophe. They must understand the relationship between the people, the systems, and the living world.

### Decisions protected or changed

- Preserve prologue puzzle designs, order, and solutions, including the White. Improve usability separately.
- Use the source's 51 units as a working sequence. Units include major puzzles, short applications, and quiet interactions; they are not 51 equally demanding puzzles.
- Preserve Mara's late physical introduction. The main experience remains solitary environmental puzzle traversal.
- Preserve the prologue's live voice and lit window. A living person is hinted early; her existence is not a midpoint surprise.
- Replace the earlier story that one breach killed the last man and ended reproduction. Humanity's decline is prolonged. The breach remains a local tragedy within that history, not the punishment that makes oppressive adults correct about freedom.
- Use the newer chronology: Mara born P.A. 302, birthday parcel at nine in P.A. 311, present P.A. 387, approximately 85 years old. The older GDD's six/82 version is superseded in this proposal.
- Mara is the last human alive in story canon, as requested. Characters infer this from dwindling shelter reports and long silence; a local terminal does not possess omniscient certainty. No last-second survivor tease.
- D17 has no hidden human soul. The parcel contains boots, not seeds that save the planet or a secret power source.
- Mara remains alive at the end. No death reveal, sacrificial outing, or fade timed to a final breath.

## 2. World bible: from allocation to inheritance

### Geography and material logic

The refuge occupies an excavated shaft beneath a terraced industrial valley. Lower rings held labour, distribution, and crowded housing; upper rings held schools, administration, medical facilities, and surface access. Retrofitted passages connect homes and service infrastructure. The ascent travels through increasingly recent occupation. Direct Works access is damaged or sealed, so the residential route is necessary rather than an arbitrary sightseeing detour.

The surface suffered a prolonged, fictional combination of contamination and ecosystem collapse. This was one refuge in a network. Long-running filtration, watershed treatment, soil work, propagation, and planting have gradually improved the surrounding region. The player visits one tangible part of that effort. No single valve cleanses a planet; no instant tree growth proves the restoration succeeded.

The forest at the ending has grown over decades. Some managed propagation beds still need irrigation; mature surrounding woodland is already establishing its own cycles. This distinction makes Mara's departure credible. D17 helps complete a local regulator and access bypass, not an entire biosphere.

These historical refinements are proposed canon. The old opening phrase “seventy one years since the breach” conflicts with the supplied dates; correct or remove that wording once the date is approved, without changing its puzzle.

Do not make one contaminant, one teenager's mistake, or one infertile person responsible for extinction. It is the fictional history of sustained global damage, declining populations, failing infrastructure, and an eventually unrecoverable human future. The game needs converging evidence, not a biomedical lecture.

### The important moral complication

The Commons did not turn off patients' air to water trees. People debated how to spend surplus capacity, reduced ambitions, maintained essential care, and found workarounds. Some wanted one more comfortable communal room; others wanted nursery expansion. A revised plan can show both on a smaller scale. A community becomes believable through negotiation and practical compromise, not a unanimous inspirational speech.

Order's survivors could be capable, kind in private, and implicated in terrible decisions. The Commons could be generous, stubborn, and inefficient. A protective machine could trap a pupil and later catch a falling courier. This complexity makes care a question of attention and context rather than a colour-coded faction.

Lower mass death gives way to respectfully cared-for individuals, then empty places, then one maintained daily routine. Loss becomes more personal as the world becomes more beautiful.

## 3. Character arcs

### D17

**Want:** deliver the parcel. **Initial limitation:** he understands help through correct execution. **Need:** care includes attention, receiving help, and staying after an assignment ends.

He has a personality from the start: precise hands, disproportionate effort, careful handling of dormant couriers, an earnest glance at a mechanism that has behaved unexpectedly. Do not begin with a blank calculator and add a soul in the last scene.

| Stage | Expressed through behaviour |
|---|---|
| Assigned function | Presents to scanners, aligns useful objects, moves on when access opens. |
| Curiosity | Lingers in Mara's seat and notices a shared drawing from her perspective. |
| Awareness of absence | Restores a dinner routine and looks toward the place it sets for someone who is gone. |
| Care beyond access | Retains his lift mechanically so its water can continue into living roots. |
| Vulnerability | The knee fails after a successful transfer; the feared attendant catches him. |
| Receiving | Sits for Mara's repair, allowing the person he came to serve to help him. |
| Reciprocity | Holds until she is ready, accepts her hold, and follows her choice. |
| Chosen presence | With no item outstanding, sits beside her anyway. |

Seed wear through safe post-prologue settling animations and a slight recovery pause. Never randomly reduce reliable jump distance. The knee failure occurs at a safe authored deposit, not as punishment for player performance.

### Mara

**Immediate want:** stabilise the last managed nursery branch and finish her access bypass. **Lifelong desire:** experience an actual tree. **Habit:** she can always find another useful repair and dislikes needing help. **Need:** accept that meaningful work can outlast her, and that her own remaining life deserves care too.

Mara is an irrigation technician. Her intelligence is practical: a feel for pressure, a way of listening to a failing fitting, an ugly repair that works. She has repaired work clothes, soil-stained hands, a modified walking support, and no patience for inaccessible bolts. She is occasionally amused, sometimes curt, and quietly playful. She is neither an endlessly smiling grandmother nor a permanent monument to grief.

Child Mara draws a world larger than the classroom. Teen Mara resents carrying adults' hopes as an obligation. Young adult Mara acts impatiently in the survey attempt and helps in its aftermath. Adult Mara works with colleagues, loves Rowan, and finds satisfaction in a real life below. Widowed Mara changes tools and habits. Old Mara still wants private things: a comfortable seat, a good cup, and to discover whether bark feels like the classroom model.

Her work can contain guilt, love, joy, competence, and habit in different proportions. Do not explain seventy years by a single trauma. D17 does not cure her through a monologue. Their practical partnership makes a difficult action possible; she takes it.

### People carried through traces

- **Parent:** the birthday gift imagines an ordinary future for one child. The sender is hopeful, not an omniscient prophet.
- **Lisle:** encourages Mara by helping her test an idea. His corrections sit beside her drawing rather than obliterating it. She asked for the placement.
- **Abel and Orme:** friends with recognisable marks. One adds extravagant creatures to drawings; the other corrects impossible mechanisms, then contributes jokes. These marks mature into adult work. Neither exists only to become a death statistic.
- **Rowan, proposed partner:** a propagation worker with personal habits and an affectionately competing view of roots and pipework. Their chipped bowl, coat, handwriting, and failed experiments must establish a person before the empty place asks us to grieve them.
- **Attendant:** follows stable protective rules. It can constrain or support without secretly becoming a villain or a philosopher. Human repair and its own pad maintenance show mutual dependence.

### Details to protect

Three children draw themselves beneath a tree too large for the paper and extend it with a strip torn from a lesson sheet. Rowan insists in a margin that roots will not obey Mara's tidy diagram. Mara's repairs have two small witness taps; when she repairs D17 she makes the same two taps with her spanner. Anonymous work gains a living rhythm.

## 5. Puzzle architecture

### Fair misdirection

A plausible wrong solution applies a true earlier rule too broadly. Feedback reveals the missing distinction. A clever player who understands immediately can solve immediately; failure is never a mandatory unlock.

Major puzzles need a central inference, meaningful experiments, and a clear causal model. Quiet interactions do not all need tricks. If every chair conceals a gotcha, the world becomes dishonest and the ending exhausting.

### Permanent rules

| System | Physical truth | Development |
|---|---|---|
| Plates and docks | Load plates measure load; keyed cradles and docking contacts have visible interfaces | Stand-in → two conditions → human seat → two physical departure controls; never intention-reading. |
| Brakes and catches | Retain a mechanically supported state at visible engagement positions | Platform → freed prop → unloaded repair → hold for another person. |
| Paired outputs | Shared drive changes outputs predictably; a slipping clutch permits one restrained branch | Blinds → furniture → shutter → shared action. |
| Routes | Junction or coupling selects a visible path; some loads must be parked or isolated first | Carrier → service → power → water → retrieval bay. |
| Water | Suspended water adds weight; a float rises by buoyancy; stored level follows inlet/outlet | Teach weight and float distinctly before combining. Elevated supply provides gravity; no free-energy loop. |
| Support transfer | A replacement takes the load before the old support is removed | Landing pin → pressure isolation → knee → handover. |
| Reactive beings | White responds to sound near water; attendant detects movement in marked zones | Diversion reasoning transfers, sensory rules do not silently become identical. |
| Partner | Holds until the other reaches a safe ready state | Machine substitute → receiving help → reciprocal support. |

### Learning ladder

| Relationship | Introduce | Develop / twist | Combine | Reframe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Movable access | DL-01–02 | DL-08 ballast becomes step | DL-22 return tray; DL-32 carrier transports link | DL-50 seat positioned for Mara |
| Persistent condition | DL-03 | DL-04 keyed courier; DL-12 coat and presence | DL-15 seat; DL-39 circuits | DL-44/47 real partner holds |
| Retain before releasing | DL-08–09 | DL-14 restrain one output | DL-19 repair; DL-29 water used twice | DL-46 safely leave work |

| Receive help | Dormant courier in DL-04 plants a visual rhyme | Attendant self-maintenance in DL-38 | Catch in DL-40, Mara repair in DL-43 | Voluntary company in DL-51 |

### Recovery and accessibility

Keep movement, crouch, jump/mantle, interact, push/pull, carry/place, present, and sit. No new final-act minigame. Keep cause and effect together in frame or connect them through a deliberate camera reveal. Shape, alignment, and motion supplement colour and sound.

Accept alternative solutions when they satisfy the established physical rules. If a player weights a floor-enable pad and then joins Mara at the other control, that can be a valid cooperative departure. Do not secretly make a formerly load-sensitive sensor recognise intent to enforce one exact choreography. Authored emotional composition must be achieved through staging and performance, not dishonest exceptions.

Checkpoint before danger and after permanent solutions. Retain braked platforms, completed transfers, docked routes, and inserted parts. Return essential props to reachable trays. A wrong hypothesis should teach within seconds, not demand replaying three solved rooms. The shared ascent has no escort health, abandonment timer, or companion deaths.

Moral complexity is in the world and relationships, not in arbitrary sacrifices. No patient loses air to grow a tree; no experimental wrong input destroys an irreplaceable plant. Safeguards show a limit, then the player finds a better distribution.

## 7. Full post-prologue plan

“Preserve” retains the source solution in substance. “Refine” changes clarity, rule, or geometry. “Rework” changes the proposed solution. Built school alterations require comparison with the actual build. Every principal card has a plausible failed hypothesis; short payoffs deliberately do not all contain one.

### DL-12 Coats Without Children

- **Disposition:** preserve; simultaneous conditions.
- **Builds on:** carry/climb, persistent load, machine stand-in.
- **Goal / evidence:** open school gate. Occupancy strip and coat peg have separate indicators on the gate; numbered coat lies in lost property.

## 9. Cinematic and emotional direction

The progression is not uniformly sad followed by uniformly happy. School play makes storage hurt; domestic warmth makes the second bowl hurt; ecological hope makes bodily limitation matter; received repair makes regained competence tender.

Alternate substantial inference, short confident application, and quiet consequence. Vary lengths rather than repeat this as a rigid three-room formula. Do not end every emotional shot the instant a puzzle flag turns green.

Wide frames express scale; closer frames express intention, support, and response. Keep object contact believable. Mara's personality lives in weight shifts, checking a fitting, waiting, restrained amusement, and touch. D17's growth is in what he notices and how long he waits, not a journal announcing compassion.

Sound begins with industrial demands and water danger, passes through mechanical lessons and domestic routines, then gains living breath and natural movement. Music need not swell at every reveal. The finale needs ordinary wind audible through its score. Check the specific mix that overwhelmed Kiki during the city reveal.

Outside is beautiful and imperfect: deadwood, uneven roots, damaged planting cages, weather, insects, recovering stream banks. No text declares everything healed or humanity forgiven. Avoid a final montage explaining what the held image already carries.

## 10. Duration, validation, and scope

**Goal:** approximately 150 minutes for a typical first-time playthrough. **Observed:** Nat, informed designer, 14-minute prologue; Kiki, unfamiliar with puzzlers, 26-minute prologue. These are two observations, not a representative distribution or a median.

The source's estimates sum to 167:30. That is a sum of labels, not a measured full game. This revision does not assign invented exact durations to unbuilt puzzles. At a 26-minute prologue, 124 minutes remain to reach 150; subtraction does not prove the remaining content provides them.

For each greybox distinguish observation, hypothesis testing, known execution, informative retry, unclear retry, optional exploration, and mandatory waiting. Five steps can take thirty seconds. Long tanks, slow carriages, repeated cranks, or confusion do not create meaningful length. Do not stretch a designer's known-solution run to 150 minutes, or cut good content because a speculative total is long.

### Prototype chains

1. **DL-12–17:** simultaneous conditions, cycle, paired outputs, human seat, full transfer, vocation. Test seat fairness and whether friends feel specific.
2. **DL-23–30:** coupling, parking, weight versus float, storage, resource used twice, living roots. Test changed-purpose comprehension without narration.
3. **DL-37–44:** capture as route, mutual maintenance, care circuit, injury, receiving repair, reciprocal work. Test whether knee scene changes feeling rather than merely speed.
4. **DL-46–51:** independent nursery, safe parking, handover, shared lift, threshold, boots, sit. Test departure as chosen life rather than abandonment or death.

Observe unfamiliar players, then ask open questions: Who was Mara before you met? Why did people keep the Works? What did the knee change? What did Mara choose? Why sit? Record misunderstanding before explaining. Puzzle success alone is not emotional validation.

### Production boundaries

One constrained traversal plane with authored depth connectors. Discrete water states, readable gravity paths, standardised rails/catches/couplings. Partner actions respond to stable authored ready states rather than require general autonomous reasoning. Large world through composition and evidence, not an open simulation. No combat, crafting, branching morality system, or late new power.

### Acceptance

Prologue remains recognisable and enjoyable after usability fixes. Principal puzzles have fair inference and useful wrong feedback. Clever players need not fail. Solves change access and understanding together. The Commons feels lived in; Mara's adulthood has joy and contradiction. Humanity's end is mourned, and its ecological care matters without redemption guarantees. Knee failure is prepared and brief; repair pays off support logic. Mara's departure is active; nursery visibly continues. Boots and sit work without explanatory speech, a death twist, or overwhelming music. Actual traversal supports length and rhythm.

### DL-01 The Wake
*Applied challenge · about 2:00 · built*

- **Builds on:** nothing. The first room.
- **New:** moving, jumping, crouching and using things; push.
- **Play:**
  1. Crouch under the fallen shutter (he does it himself) and heave its T-handle twice: the latch gives, the counterweight drops, the shutter swings up.
  2. Jump the fallen conveyor: the first jump.
  3. The drain trench is too wide to jump and too deep to climb out of. Push the depot cart to its lip and in: it becomes the bridge.
  4. Crank the bulkhead wheel, five strokes, and walk through.
- **Wrong first try:** drop into the trench and try to jump out. He flinches and is back at the checkpoint, unharmed.
- **The aha:** the cart is a floor you can move, not an obstacle.
- **Carries forward:** DL-03 (the trolley is pushed), DL-08 (an object as a step), DL-10 (a crank winds a door), DL-11 (push a duct into the water), DL-49 (a heavy latch opened together).

### DL-02 The Sorting Hall
*Applied challenge · about 1:45 · built*

- **Builds on:** DL-01 (push, climb, a moving thing to read before you pass it).
- **New:** carry and set down.
- **Play:**
  1. Pass the franking press while it is up: its lamp blinks before each stroke.
  2. The table top is just out of a jump's reach. Lift the parcel box by its strap, carry it to the table's foot, set it down.
  3. Box, table, gantry, the lit door.
  4. Off the path: the tube drops a letter; post it at the brass slot (the chapter's secret verb, taught with nothing at stake).
- **Wrong first try:** jump for the table from the floor. It nearly works.
- **The aha:** objects can be moved to where you need a step.
- **Carries forward:** DL-03 (carry a weight), DL-09 (a hazard on a cycle), DL-10 (carry a passenger), DL-12 (carry while climbing), DL-13 (a cycle to wait for).

### DL-03 The Weight of the Job
*Developed puzzle · about 2:30 · built*

- **Builds on:** DL-02 (carry and set down), DL-01 (push).
- **New:** a plate holds a gate open only while something is on it, and something else can be that something.
- **Play:**
  1. Stand on the plate: the gate rises. Step off: it drops. The gauge on the post shows the load.
  2. Neither the sorting weight nor the trolley alone is heavy enough.
  3. Carry the weight onto the trolley's deck, push the trolley onto the plate.
  4. Climb over the trolley and walk under the gate.
- **Wrong first try:** stand on the plate and run for the gate. It drops before he gets there.
- **The aha:** he doesn't have to be the weight. Two light things together make one heavy one.
- **Carries forward:** DL-04 (the next plate wants a courier), DL-05 (the right amount), DL-07 (the bucket), DL-08 (load on a pan), DL-12 (two conditions), DL-48 (two travellers on a plate).

### DL-04 The Racks
*Developed puzzle · about 2:30 · built*

- **Builds on:** DL-03 (a plate, a stand-in).
- **New:** drag; the plate reads what kind of thing is on it.
- **Play:**
  1. The bay's plate carries a courier pictogram. Standing on it opens the door; stepping off drops it, as in DL-03.
  2. The trolley can't follow him: it is holding the gate behind.
  3. Take the fallen courier by its tow loop and drag it, walking backwards, to the plate.
  4. The cradle draws it in; its lamps flicker and hold; the door opens.
- **Wrong first try:** go back for the trolley. Taking it off the plate would shut the gate behind him.
- **The aha:** the bay wants one of his own. Carrying becomes care.
- **Carries forward:** DL-05 (drag a body to a plate), DL-10 (a stand-in for a person), DL-12 (a figure as a pupil), DL-34 (a dummy that stands in for him).

### DL-05 The Ration Hall
*Developed puzzle · about 2:30 · built*

- **Builds on:** DL-03 (a plate holds a state), DL-04 (drag, and a plate that reads what kind of thing is on it), DL-01 (push).
- **New:** the plate wants the right amount: not too little, not too much.
- **Play:**
  1. Stand on the weighbridge: the needle barely moves. UNDER, and a buzzer.
  2. Push the ration drum onto it: the needle slams to OVER, a bell. Push it on, past the plate, out of the way.
  3. Behind where the drum stood, one of the dead, a rope round the ankles. Drag it onto the plate.
  4. The needle settles in PERSON; the hatch winds up and stays. Crouch under it.
- **Wrong first try:** the drum: heavier must be better, after DL-03.
- **The aha:** the hatch counts heads, not weight. The plate wants a person, and the room is full of them.
- **Carries forward:** DL-10 (the car weighs passengers), DL-15 (seats that count), DL-50 (enough, not perfect).

### DL-06 Return to Sender
*Applied challenge · about 2:45 · built*

- **Builds on:** DL-01 (levers act at a distance), DL-02 (read a moving machine before stepping in).
- **New:** routing: stop, switch, restart, ride.
- **Play:**
  1. The scanner refuses his label. Carriers rise from the return hatch, stop at the dock and are hauled to the chute.
  2. Board one: it tips him into the pile of dead letters, and he is back at the scanner.
  3. Stop the belt, throw the points, restart the belt: a chase of green lamps runs up the ramp.
  4. Board at the dock and ride up.
- **Wrong first try:** ride the carrier as it comes. It goes where the system sends it.
- **The aha:** the route is a setting, and he can change it.
- **Carries forward:** DL-07 (send something else), DL-16 (route moving pupils), DL-22 (route a trolley), DL-25 (route power), DL-28 (the works' carriers), DL-37 (route the attendant).

### DL-07 The Heap
*Developed puzzle · about 2:30 · built*

- **Builds on:** DL-06 (routing, boarding), DL-03 (a weight holds a state), DL-02 (carry).
- **New:** a counterweight: weight in a bucket over here lifts a gate over there.
- **Play:**
  1. Ride up, for nothing: a gate closes the gallery, its chain running to a bucket at the ramp's head. Slide back down.
  2. Carry a sorting weight into a carrier at the dock and send it up the ramp: it tips into the bucket, and the gate lifts halfway, straining.
  3. Fetch the second weight from the far end and send it too: the gate opens.
  4. Ride the next carrier up and walk under the gate.
- **Wrong first try:** ride up and push at the gate.
- **The aha:** he doesn't have to be there. The system can deliver the weight for him.
- **Carries forward:** DL-09 (a counterweight to balance), DL-10 (ride a counterweight), DL-26 (water as the weight), DL-29 (the weight is the delivery).

### DL-08 The Handbrake
*Developed puzzle · about 3:00 · built*

- **Builds on:** DL-03 (load holds a state), DL-01 (push, a latch), DL-07 (weight here lifts something there).
- **New:** a pawl that bites only at the right height; walking quietly by the water.
- **Play:**
  1. Load on the pan raises the platform. D-17 alone raises it halfway; push the ballast trolley onto the pan and it rises all the way.
  2. Step onto the platform as it is and it drops under him: he scrambles back.
  3. Throw the pawl: below the landing it rattles on the rack; at the landing it drops home with a click.
  4. With the pawl set, the platform doesn't need the trolley. Push the trolley off the pan, across the platform to the far rail, and climb it as a step.
  5. Walk, don't run, over the shaft: the White slaps the water if he is loud. Then the returns belt, against him.
- **Wrong first try:** leave the trolley on the pan, as every scene so far taught. Then the far rail is too high.
- **The aha:** the latch frees the weight for another job: the weight becomes the step.
- **Carries forward:** DL-09 (the pawl against time), DL-11 (the White hunts by sound), DL-13 (a brake stops a cycle), DL-19 (replace a support under load), DL-28 (latch a carrier).

### DL-09 The Slipping Cage
*Set piece · about 2:00 · built*

- **Builds on:** DL-07 (counterweight), DL-08 (the pawl), DL-02 (carry; a hazard on a cycle).
- **New:** balance read from a gauge; a latch thrown in a moving window.
- **Play:**
  1. The cage dips under him and hauls itself back: the gauge reads too light.
  2. Carry the two weight plates to the counterweight's tray: each moves the needle halfway. Balanced, the cage takes him.
  3. It lets down, shudders and slips. It catches once above the lit landing: the lamp blinks faster and the chevrons line up. Pull the pawl then.
  4. Cross the cable's two arcs in turn, waiting on the dry hatch between them.
- **Wrong first try:** pull the pawl the moment the cage slips. It rattles and springs back.
- **The aha:** the landing tells you when: the lamp and the chevrons are the timing.
- **Carries forward:** DL-10 (the gauge again, and the counterweight ridden), DL-13 (a dial shows the moment), DL-29 (brake a lift at the top), DL-32 (cross phased platforms), DL-45 (a latched counterweight), DL-48 (the last lift).

### DL-10 Up Only
*Developed puzzle · about 2:30 · built*

- **Builds on:** DL-05 (the plate wants a person), DL-09 (the gauge, the counterweight), DL-02 (carry), DL-01 (crank a door).
- **New:** he takes the counterweight's place.
- **Play:**
  1. Press UP: the car is empty, and the panel buzzes.
  2. Carry the suitcase tagged C-114 into the car: the plate dips, the gauge swings to balanced, the gate shuts. UP now sends the car up and back without him.
  3. Crank the service door open: behind it, the counterweight's shaft and its own UP.
  4. Stand on the counterweight and press its UP: the car rises with the suitcase, and he rides down to ring C.
- **Wrong first try:** ride in the car with the suitcase. It only goes up.
- **The aha:** if the car goes up, the counterweight comes down. Be the counterweight.
- **Carries forward:** DL-12 (an object as a pupil), DL-29 (ride a water counterweight), DL-39 (be carried with a part), DL-48 (the lift that takes two).

### DL-11 The Child at the Address
*Developed puzzle, then quiet · about 3:30 · built*

- **Builds on:** DL-08 (the White hears noise; walk), DL-01 (push into a gap), DL-04 (drag), DL-06 (the scanner reads his label).
- **New:** a lure: a louder noise draws the White away; present the parcel.
- **Play:**
  1. Walk the flooded lane: the first time it hears him, the White surfaces and hisses, a warning.
  2. Ring C-108's doorbell: for twelve seconds the White noses at that step.
  3. While it rings, push the fallen duct into the narrow point: the splash doesn't turn it. Walk across.
  4. Knock at C-114; pull the child's cord. Drag the fallen desk clear of the terminal.
  5. Present the parcel: NO ONE AT THIS ADDRESS. FORWARD TO SCHOOL, RING B.
- **Wrong first try:** push the duct first. The splash brings the White straight to him.
- **The aha:** a noise he makes on purpose, somewhere else, buys the quiet he needs here.
- **Carries forward:** DL-15 (a seat to take), DL-17 (forwarding again), DL-33 (a terminal), DL-35 (lure the attendant with the dummy), DL-42 (present to a person), DL-51 (deliver).

## Addendum, 23 October 2026: the present-day service thread (approved)

From the post-prologue design review, approved by the director. After the school entrance, one consistent present-day
thread runs through the journey: the prologue's **new cable** (glossy black, bright galvanised clips, taped joints, new
splice and relay boxes with green pilot lamps). It is Mara's ongoing maintenance. Its relays and pumps cycle on their
own; changes follow equipment cycles or work being done elsewhere, never D-17's arrival (she doesn't know a courier is
coming). Introduce it in the school as a recent intervention beside obsolete routines (DL-12's splice into the old
supply; the relay boxes; DL-17's refill pump, from which it goes on up the stair to ring A); repeat it in a changed
context in the homes (selected service lines kept and repurposed while personal routines were retired); reveal its
ecological destination in the Works; confirm its author through her working habits and the repaired circuit in the
workshop. Don't attach it to every scene: each recurrence must add a relationship, not repeat a symbol. Each chapter
should carry a live question in and out (the review's table, section 5.2).

## Addendum, 23 October 2026: the director's perspective

From the director's answers to the perspective questions, approved for the story board. Where this disagrees with
sections 1 to 9, this wins.

### The theme

**Nothing stays: not the refuge, not its people, not the tree. What we get is the time in between, and it is best
spent close to someone: mending what's near, making room for what comes next, and playing for no reason at all.**

Three strands, always together:
- **Endings are part of the pattern.** Passing is not failure. Humanity is the old tree; the world grows back around
  it. The game mourns and does not despair.
- **Loneliness is the worst of fates.** Mara's nine years alone are the real danger, not extinction. The climax is not
  the delivery but someone arriving who stays. Protection that isolates (Order, the protocols) is the same enemy in a
  kinder coat.
- **Whimsy is joy for its own sake,** and it is the game's signature. D-17 plays when nothing asks him to. People are
  wonderful and weird, and the world shows it.

What the game argues against: noble sacrifice. Care matters, but it cannot eat a life; Mara leaves work unfinished
because her one life matters too. Don't change the world: change what's around you.

### Mara's calling

Mara mends and she grows, and to her they are one act: **keeping company with something until it can go on without
her.** She learned it by being mended herself: a toy patched, sleeves let down year after year, a parent's "keep
drawing your trees". Repair is how she was loved; growing things is how she was hoped for. So she keeps the old going
as long as it can go (pumps, circuits, the old tree she draws) and makes room for the new to start (the nursery).
Alone, her repairs have become her company: she names the pumps, logs her work to nobody, chats to seedlings. Her
habit is one more thing to mend. Her need is to let someone mend her, then to sit.

### D-17: persistence, whimsy, and making room

D-17 persists, and he chooses whimsy because he can: the hopscotch on the way to C-114 is the signature moment. Give
every chapter one moment of play that no puzzle needs. His second quirk grows across the game: a courier's habit of
stepping aside (for the dormant courier, the procession, the attendant seating its pad) becomes care. At the end he
steps back while Mara opens the boots, giving her room, before she makes room for him.

### The survey: beautiful and consequential

The last children rebelled against protection that kept them safe and alone. In P.A. 319 Mara, Abel and Orme went
outside without leave, to see a real tree. Both things are true at once:
- **What they brought back:** a tin of soil and two seedlings, the nursery's first tray, and a photograph of a grey
  field with one dead, gnarled tree in it. Mara knew it at once: the crooked tree she had always drawn. The
  restoration began here.
- **What they gave:** Orme, the careful one who checked every seal and came anyway, breathed the outside air when his
  mask's seal failed. He lived, never went outside again, and breathed through a filter for the rest of his life (he
  built the nursery's first air filters). The three were held a season in quarantine, in separate rooms: the custody
  wards. Mara's guardian, who could not let go, never quite forgave her, and Mara's apology was never sent.

Nobody died because of them. The adults were not villains: they were terrified, right about the danger and wrong
about the cage. Rebellion is not punished and not glorified; it is beautiful and it costs.

In the wards: the survey kit replaces the "breach tools"; the line was the guardian hauling them back; the soil jar is
dated; the three friends tapped to each other through the pipes (the cup at the pipe, from the school refuge).

### The tree

One dead tree, woody, gnarled and almost haunted, a great branch long fallen, standing alone in a wild field brimming
with light and wildflowers. The player watches the world reclaim it over time, out of order: Mara's yearly drawings
through the Works' sampling periscope (grass, then wildflowers, then saplings at its foot, then a vine up the trunk),
and late, in the ward, the survey's photograph of the grey field where it began. Outside, it stands among young
growth. Mara sits beneath it. Humanity is the old tree; the world grows back around it.

### Human weirdness

Lean on it everywhere: quirks, odd hobbies, jokes in margins, personal rules. A mosaic of bottle caps sorted by
colour; rota margins full of jokes; a council rule, *Argue with the plan, not the person*; the last party room with
every cup washed ("Last ones out do the washing up"); two stools and a homemade rod by the reservoir with a tally of
fish caught (none); Lisle's bench sign, *Break it here, not out there*.
