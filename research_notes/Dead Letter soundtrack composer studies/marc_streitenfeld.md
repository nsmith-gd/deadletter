## Background and how it shaped his approach

### Takeaway
Streitenfeld is a largely self-taught German composer (b. Munich, 1974). He came up through Hans Zimmer's studio and then worked as a music editor and supervisor on Ridley Scott films before Scott made him a composer. He has no academic training, and he spent years editing music against picture. That likely explains his sound: texture first, built from sounds he makes himself, with sparse thematic material.

### Cited Findings
- Born in Munich in 1974. Largely self-taught. Moved to Los Angeles at 19. His first film-industry job was as an assistant to Hans Zimmer. — [Wikipedia: Marc Streitenfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Streitenfeld); [GRAMMY.com interview](https://www.grammy.com/news/video-marc-streitenfeld-is-settling-the-score/)
- During the Zimmer years he played guitar on *Crimson Tide* and gave technical score advice on *Gladiator* and *The Thin Red Line*. This gave him extensive experience working with orchestras. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Streitenfeld); [marcstreitenfeld.com biography](https://marcstreitenfeld.com/biography/)
- In the early 2000s he worked as a music editor and music supervisor, on *Mission: Impossible II*, *Black Hawk Down* and *Kingdom of Heaven*. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Streitenfeld)
- His composing career began when Ridley Scott asked him to score *A Good Year* (2006). Scott then hired him for *American Gangster* (2007), *Body of Lies* (2008), *Robin Hood* (2010) and *Prometheus* (2012). Other credits: *The Grey* (2012, Joe Carnahan), *Poltergeist* (2015), *All I See Is You* (2016/17), and Scott's HBO Max series *Raised by Wolves*. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Streitenfeld)
- He won "Discovery of the Year" at the World Soundtrack Awards for *American Gangster*. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Streitenfeld)
- Process (his own account): he reads the script, sometimes visits the set, and sees rough cuts before he writes. — [GRAMMY.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/video-marc-streitenfeld-is-settling-the-score/)
- He does not use sample libraries: "I make all the sounds you hear in my scores." He says he spends a lot of time creating textures and percussive sounds. — [Variety, 2015, "Composer Marc Streitenfeld Is Arduous In His Search for the Lost Chord"](https://variety.com/2015/music/features/composer-marc-streitenfeld-is-arduous-in-his-search-for-the-lost-chord-1201501614/)
- A fan wiki says he has recorded many of his scores at Abbey Road Studios in London. — [Ultimate Pop Culture wiki](https://ultimatepopculture.fandom.com/wiki/Marc_Streitenfeld) (secondary/fan source)
- Critics doubted his credentials. Movie Music UK, reviewing *Prometheus*: "Having only scored seven films since making his debut in 2006, there was really nothing in his filmography to suggest that he had the composing chops necessary to step up to the task." — [Movie Music UK](https://moviemusicuk.us/2012/06/20/prometheus-marc-streitenfeld/)
- **"Welcome to Marwen" is not a Streitenfeld score.** *Welcome to Marwen* (2018, Robert Zemeckis) was composed and conducted by Alan Silvestri. — [Wikipedia: Welcome to Marwen (soundtrack)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welcome_to_Marwen_(soundtrack)); [Film Music Reporter](https://filmmusicreporter.com/2018/12/13/welcome-to-marwen-soundtrack-details/)

### Inferences
- He worked as a music editor (cutting temp tracks and fitting music to picture for Scott) before he composed for Scott. That likely trained him to think in terms of the cut and the overall sound mix rather than in terms of themes. This would explain why critics keep describing his scores as effective in the film but thin as albums (see the critical reception section). This is an inference; no source states the causal link.
- Because he was self-taught and built his own sounds rather than using libraries, his "orchestration" is partly done in the recording and processing stage. The lesson for a game composer: record source material, then treat it as raw matter to transform.
- The sources say only "assistant to Hans Zimmer". None of them confirms that he was at Media Ventures or Remote Control specifically. In that era Zimmer's studio was Media Ventures, so "Media Ventures" is the likelier label, but this is unverified.

### Gaps
- No source found on his formal or informal training, or on which instruments he plays beyond the guitar on *Crimson Tide*.
- No technique detail found on *A Good Year* (2006). Nothing was retrieved on its instrumentation or reception.
- *All I See Is You* and *Raised by Wolves*: no score descriptions found. Composer credit-sharing on *Raised by Wolves* (e.g., with Ben Frost) is unverified.

## Prometheus (2012): textures, backwards recording, Gregson-Williams' theme, awe/dread/alien

### Takeaway
*Prometheus* is his signature experimental score. He wrote orchestral parts out backwards, recorded a 90-piece orchestra at Abbey Road playing them, then digitally reversed the recordings. The result is his own melodies heard "forward" but with the uncanny swells and decays of reversed sound, which he called "unusual, unsettling". He spent months recording and processing source sounds, flutes and woodwinds especially. The broad, awe-inspiring "Life" theme heard at key moments was written by Harry Gregson-Williams, and reviewers say it displaced much of Streitenfeld's own thematic material in the finished film.

### Cited Findings
**Backwards recording (composer's own words)**
- "I actually wrote out the sheet music backwards so the orchestra played it backwards and then I digitally flipped it." — quoted in search summaries of [Static Mass interview](http://staticmass.net/exclusive-interviews/marc-streitenfeld-prometheus-composer/) and [Movie Music UK](https://moviemusicuk.us/2012/06/20/prometheus-marc-streitenfeld/)
- "So you're hearing the score as it's written, the same melody, but with a backwards sounding orchestra which gives it a kind of unusual, unsettling sound." — [Static Mass interview](http://staticmass.net/exclusive-interviews/marc-streitenfeld-prometheus-composer/)
- He phrased the music differently in the recording "so when you flip it there's a different expression and dynamic". He says he tried similar treatment on "pretty much everything" he recorded. — [Static Mass](http://staticmass.net/exclusive-interviews/marc-streitenfeld-prometheus-composer/); [Xenopedia/AvP Fandom wiki](https://avp.fandom.com/wiki/Prometheus_(soundtrack))
- On flutes and woodwinds: "a lot of it was also with flutes, we first started doing it with woodwinds and it was just an interesting sound". And: "Ridley really liked those sounds." — [Static Mass](http://staticmass.net/exclusive-interviews/marc-streitenfeld-prometheus-composer/)
- For *Prometheus* he "spent months just recording things" to make it sound unusual. — [Variety 2015](https://variety.com/2015/music/features/composer-marc-streitenfeld-is-arduous-in-his-search-for-the-lost-chord-1201501614/)
- A staccato pulse in the score is described as like a mechanical heartbeat. Streitenfeld described it as the sound of "blood rushing through the ears". — search summary attributing this to [Static Mass](http://staticmass.net/exclusive-interviews/marc-streitenfeld-prometheus-composer/) / [Movie Music UK](https://moviemusicuk.us/2012/06/20/prometheus-marc-streitenfeld/) (exact attribution unclear; verify)
- A review describes a recurring motif carried by what sounds like a processed bass flute. — reported in search summary; source page unconfirmed (likely [Movie Music UK](https://moviemusicuk.us/2012/06/20/prometheus-marc-streitenfeld/)). A later search could not confirm it, so treat it as unverified.

**Recording logistics**
- Recorded over about one week with a 90-piece orchestra at Abbey Road Studios, London. Conducted by Ben Foster. — [Wikipedia: Prometheus (soundtrack)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prometheus_(soundtrack)); [Banquet Records listing](https://www.banquetrecords.com/marc-streitenfeld/prometheus---ost/MOVATM290F)
- Wikipedia also says the score was "processed by Scott after the fact". The search summary leaves unclear whether this means Ridley Scott processed or reshaped it, or that the music was processed afterwards. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prometheus_(soundtrack))

**Gregson-Williams' "Life" theme**
- Harry Gregson-Williams contributed two pieces to the album: "Life" (track 4, 2:33) and "We Were Right" (track 12, 2:45). He was not credited in the film itself, only on the album. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prometheus_(soundtrack)); [AvP Fandom](https://avp.fandom.com/wiki/Prometheus_(soundtrack))
- Movie Music UK: much of Streitenfeld's own thematic material was replaced in the finished film by Gregson-Williams' music. "Life" is the standout and recurs at key moments. Rated three stars. — [Movie Music UK](https://moviemusicuk.us/2012/06/20/prometheus-marc-streitenfeld/)
- "Life" and "We Were Right" were later quoted in Jed Kurzel's *Alien: Covenant* score. — [Wikipedia: Alien: Covenant (soundtrack)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alien:_Covenant_(soundtrack))
- A Norman Records customer review says the Gregson-Williams theme is good but "doesn't steal the show". — [Norman Records](https://www.normanrecords.com/records/159627-marc-streitenfeld-prometheus-original-motion-picture-soundtrack) (customer review, low weight)

**Track-level details (named in sources)**

- "Debris" and "Planting the Seed" present the Creature theme "in all its Goldenthal like glory with synth stingers and growling brass under sliding strings and shrill woodwinds". "Hammerpede" is listed among the action cues. — [Soundtrack Universe](https://soundtrack-universe.blogspot.com/2017/03/prometheus-review-re-uploaded.html)
- "Engineers" and "Hammerpede" feature choir per the album credits. — [YouTube Music album listing](https://music.youtube.com/playlist?list=OLAK5uy_nDLvrHCSQUSyzDzWCd3b2sigOf10xjZuk) (via search summary)
- "Not Human" is on the tracklist. No review commentary was found on it. — [Discogs](https://www.discogs.com/master/556732-Marc-Streitenfeld-Prometheus-Original-Motion-Picture-Soundtrack)
- "Friend from the Past" reprises Jerry Goldsmith's main title from *Alien*. — record-store listing summarised in search ([Tower Records IE](https://towerrecords.ie/products/marc-streitenfeld-ost-prometheus))

**Critical reception**
- Filmtracks (June 2012): the score introduces four new themes that do not form a clear narrative. Sound design intrudes on the suspense and horror cues. The score is a tribute to earlier Alien composers that leans into melodrama, and Filmtracks recommends the album mainly to Alien fans. Sources disagree on its star rating (Wikipedia shows 1 star; a fan wiki says 3/5). — [Filmtracks](https://www.filmtracks.com/titles/prometheus.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prometheus_(soundtrack)); [Ultimate Pop Culture wiki](https://ultimatepopculture.fandom.com/wiki/Prometheus_(soundtrack))
- Movie Music UK called it a difficult assignment that Streitenfeld "didn't quite meet". — [Movie Music UK](https://moviemusicuk.us/2012/06/20/prometheus-marc-streitenfeld/)
- Soundtrack Universe called it "rather good". — [Soundtrack Universe](https://soundtrack-universe.blogspot.com/2017/03/prometheus-review-re-uploaded.html)

### Inferences
- **How the reversal creates "alien" feeling.** Reversed acoustic sound puts the attack at the end of each note: swells into a sudden cut-off, with reverb that blooms before the note. If the melody is written in reverse, it plays back right-way-round, so the listener hears a recognisable, even lyrical line whose physics are wrong. That is familiar and strange at once, which suits a story about humanity's makers. This explains the mechanism; it is not a claim about how specific cues sound.
- **Division of labour.** In the film, awe (the broad, noble "Life" theme by Gregson-Williams) and dread (Streitenfeld's reversed and processed textures, the Creature theme with synth stingers and growling brass) come from different hands. For a game this suggests a two-layer system: one clear, hummable "wonder" theme kept for revelation moments, over a large bank of unstable, processed texture for exploration and threat.
- **Woodwinds as the main texture source.** Flutes and woodwinds are breath-driven and noisy, so they reverse more strangely than bowed strings. The bass-flute motif (unverified) fits this.
- Filmtracks complained that the sound design intrudes. For a game, this blurring of score and sound effects is an asset rather than a flaw, because adaptive audio benefits when the two are hard to tell apart.

### Gaps
- No primary source was read on which specific cues use the backwards technique.
- No confirmed descriptions of aleatoric or atonal string writing, or of drones in specific cues, beyond the "sliding strings" in "Debris" and "Planting the Seed".
- No making-of or liner-note text was retrieved. The Bandcamp page ([marc-streitenfeld.bandcamp.com](https://marc-streitenfeld.bandcamp.com/)) might hold liner notes.
- A Variety piece mentions guitar-based siren sounds and a 1969 drum machine, but the summary did not tie them to a specific score (possibly *Poltergeist*). Not attributed.

## The Grey (2012): minimalism, restraint, grief, silence and wilderness

### Takeaway
Joe Carnahan wanted a minimal underscore, and he and Streitenfeld left much of the film unscored. Alaskan wind and other sound effects fill those stretches. When music does enter, it is mostly strings with muted piano and keyboards. Two themes sit at the album's start and end. Separately, there are "truly creepy instrumental techniques" for the wolves. Critics found it intelligent in the film but hard to listen to on its own.

### Cited Findings
- Filmtracks: the director's brief was for a minimal underscore. Strings get "primary duty", performing the composer's only thematic material "alongside tastefully muted keyboarding". The accessible parts come at the beginning and end of the album, and both state Streitenfeld's two themes. — [Filmtracks: The Grey](https://www.filmtracks.com/titles/grey.html)
- Filmtracks: the music alternately frightens with "truly creepy instrumental techniques" for the wolves and urges listeners "toward gloom with dreary dramatic sequences". Filmtracks credits the "intelligence of his motific development" but notes the score's weak listenability. — [Filmtracks](https://www.filmtracks.com/titles/grey.html)
- Tracksounds: Carnahan and Streitenfeld left much of the film unscored, relying mainly on the Alaskan wind and other sound effects. — [Tracksounds](http://blog.tracksounds.com/2012/02/grey-soundtrack-by-marc-streitenfeld.html)
- Movie-Wave (James Southall): largely orchestral, strings doing the bulk of the work, emphasising the horror "more than anything". Effective in the film, but "not a particularly engrossing album". — [Movie-Wave](https://www.movie-wave.net/the-grey/)
- Louder Than War: built on light strings and piano. — [Louder Than War](https://louderthanwar.com/the-grey-ost-marc-streitenfeld-album-review/)
- Assignment X: ethereal orchestral textures, a violin, and "chilling, high-pitched tones". — [Assignment X](https://www.assignmentx.com/2012/cd-review-the-grey-soundtrack/)
- The Film Stage, on the film's sound design: "jarring and ear-piercing", most effective in the plane crash. With each splice, the film cuts out the loud sound of a broken airlock, which makes its return more frightening. — [The Film Stage](https://thefilmstage.com/review-the-grey/)
- Album: Lakeshore Records, released January 24, 2012 (Filmtracks gives February 14, 2012). 35:09 long. Orchestrated and conducted by Ben Foster. The closing track is "Into the Fray" (track 16, about 1:49–1:50). — [Filmtracks](https://www.filmtracks.com/titles/grey.html); [Deezer](https://www.deezer.com/en/album/39376831); [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/Grey-Marc-Streitenfeld/dp/B006XMQKYY)
- Shazam credits Caroline Dale (cello) and Thomas Bowes (violin and orchestra leader) on "Into the Fray". — [Shazam](https://www.shazam.com/song/495474504/into-the-fray) (credits metadata; no review confirms that the score is cello-led)

### Inferences
- **Silence as a scoring decision.** Leaving long stretches to wind and sound effects makes the wilderness the main "score". Music then reads as grief or memory breaking in, not as commentary. For a game, use the environmental ambience as the default bed and bring music in rarely, so that each entry carries meaning.
- **Two registers.** There are two separate musical languages: grief and memory (string themes, muted keys and piano, warm) and predator threat (extended techniques, high-pitched tones). A game could map them to two states, with no melodic link between them.
- **Muting as restraint.** "Tastefully muted keyboarding" and "light strings and piano" suggest low dynamics and soft attacks: a performance style, not only an orchestration choice.
- Unverified (general knowledge only): the closing track title "Into the Fray" echoes a poem recited in the film ("Once more into the fray…"). No source retrieved here confirms it.

### Gaps
- No Streitenfeld interview about *The Grey* was found. A search specifically for one came back empty.
- No track-by-track analysis was found. Only "Into the Fray" is confirmed by name.
- No source describes exactly what the wolf "creepy instrumental techniques" are (e.g., col legno, harmonics, processed sounds).

## Robin Hood (2010): folk instrumentation, medieval colour, action vs intimacy

### Takeaway
A large orchestra and choir are coloured by period and regional instruments (lutes, fiddles, uilleann pipes, hurdy-gurdy, rebec, whistles, ethnic percussion), with no synths or electric guitars. Under the action, though, sit driving, rock-like 21st-century rhythms. Reception was among his most divided. Filmtracks: avoid it. Movie-Wave: "a fine guilty pleasure", perhaps his best. The Hollywood Reporter called it brilliant.

### Cited Findings
- Instrumentation: a large orchestra and choir, plus lutes, fiddles, uilleann pipes, hurdy-gurdy, whistles and ethnic percussion. No synthetic tones or electric guitars. — [Filmtracks: Robin Hood](https://www.filmtracks.com/titles/robin_hood10.html)
- OUP blog: notes the rebec and hurdy-gurdy alongside the lute. The score has a "vaguely Celtic sound, perhaps from the uilleann pipes". The "driving rock rhythms" in the action sequences are "pure 21st century". — [OUP Blog, "Will the new Robin Hood Score ... score?"](https://blog.oup.com/2010/05/robin-hood-score/)
- Hurdy-gurdy player Nigel Eaton appears in search results connected to this score (Wikipedia's list of recordings featuring the hurdy-gurdy). The summary did not explicitly confirm his participation. — [Wikipedia: Nigel Eaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigel_Eaton); [Recordings featuring the hurdy-gurdy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recordings_featuring_the_hurdy-gurdy)
- 22-track album, released 11 May 2010. Tracks named in sources: "Fate Has Smiled Upon Us" (2:02), "Merry Men", "Pact Sworn in Blood". — [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/robin-hood-2010-original-motion-picture-soundtrack--mw0001981004); [Zanobard Reviews](https://zanobardreviews.com/2018/09/16/robin-hood-2010-soundtrack-review/)
- Zanobard (6/10): "surprisingly good", with "inspiring and very heroic music" for Robin. "Fate Has Smiled Upon Us" and "Merry Men" are "far too short". The villain theme is "fairly dull as well as underused". — [Zanobard Reviews](https://zanobardreviews.com/2018/09/16/robin-hood-2010-soundtrack-review/)
- Filmtracks: underwhelming on album without the images, lacking "true charisma, romance, and enthusiasm". — [Filmtracks](https://www.filmtracks.com/titles/robin_hood10.html)
- Maintitles.net: 2.72/5 average (32 votes). Its review says the score's simplicity and lack of orchestration and depth held it back. — [maintitles.net](https://www.maintitles.net/reviews/robin-hood/)
- James Southall (Movie-Wave): "a fine guilty pleasure", probably the best of Streitenfeld's career at that point. — [Movie-Wave](https://www.movie-wave.net/robin-hood/)
- Kirk Honeycutt (The Hollywood Reporter) praised the score as brilliant. — via [Wikipedia: Robin Hood (2010 soundtrack)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Hood_(2010_soundtrack))

### Inferences
- **Folk instruments as colour, not structure.** The period instruments add timbre on top of an orchestra driven by modern rhythm. This is the Zimmer-school hybrid approach without the synths. For a game, the transferable idea is a regional or folk solo colour (drone instruments like hurdy-gurdy or pipes) laid over a modern rhythmic engine for action, with the same solo instruments heard alone for intimate scenes.
- Hurdy-gurdy and uilleann pipes both have built-in drones. This ties *Robin Hood* to his drone-heavy writing elsewhere, though no source makes that link.

### Gaps
- No source describes specific action versus intimate cues track by track. "Action vs intimacy" handling is inferred only from instrumentation and the OUP comment on rock rhythms.
- No Streitenfeld quote about *Robin Hood* was found.

## American Gangster (2007) and Body of Lies (2008): tension writing and score vs source music

### Takeaway
*American Gangster* combines a 1970s Blaxploitation and jazz-inflected "urban cool" score with heavy use of period source songs. Additional score and source-like cues came from Hank Shocklee (Public Enemy's Bomb Squad producer), and Streitenfeld pre-recorded acoustic material. *Body of Lies* builds tension from brooding strings over almost constant percussion, with a Middle Eastern colour from the saz.

### Cited Findings
**American Gangster**
- Movie Music UK (3.5 stars): inspired by Blaxploitation composers such as Isaac Hayes and Curtis Mayfield, with "jazz textures on top". It "won't suit all tastes" but "has its moments". — [Movie Music UK](https://moviemusicuk.us/2007/11/02/american-gangster-marc-streitenfeld/)
- Filmtracks: the most effective and engrossing of his Ridley Scott scores to that point. The film first got only a compilation (songs) album. After a BAFTA nomination, Varèse Sarabande released over 30 minutes of Streitenfeld's score plus additional Shocklee source cues. — [Filmtracks](https://www.filmtracks.com/titles/american_gangster.html)
- Streitenfeld pre-recorded acoustic materials, and additional score was composed and recorded by Hank Shocklee. — [Wikipedia: American Gangster (soundtrack)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Gangster_(soundtrack))
- Shocklee is credited on tracks including "Afro Beat" and "Hallway". Harry Garfield is credited on "Back To Bangkok Blues". Sources disagree on how many Shocklee tracks there are: maintitles.net says three, kinoafisha lists two. — [kinoafisha](https://www.kinoafisha.info/en/movies/1180035/music/); [maintitles.net forum](https://www.maintitles.net/forum/discussion/167/)
- Dissenting views: Ray Bennett called the music "nothing to write home about". A maintitles.net user said it "could have been SO much more creative". — via search summary ([maintitles.net](https://www.maintitles.net/forum/discussion/167/))
- There is a Cinemablend interview with Streitenfeld about this period, but it was not retrieved. — [Cinemablend](https://cinemablend.com/soundtracks/INTERVIEW-Marc-Streitenfeld-7411.html)

**Body of Lies**
- An Amazon listener review: his third solo score and "his best and most complete work". Tension "builds through brooding string arrangements put on top of almost constant percussion". The reviewer adds that if you listened to *American Gangster* and *Body of Lies* back to back "you would be able to pick up his style immediately". — [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/Body-Lies-Marc-Streitenfeld/dp/B001FBSMX0) (user review, low weight)
- Movie Music UK: the score "quickly loses its way" as a standalone listen. It singles out the saz solo in "Dead Sea" as a highlight. — [Movie Music UK](https://moviemusicuk.us/2008/10/10/body-of-lies-marc-streitenfeld/)
- Album on Varèse Sarabande, 45:06. — [Wikipedia: Body of Lies (soundtrack)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_of_Lies_(soundtrack))

### Inferences
- **Blurring score and source music.** In *American Gangster*, score (with acoustic pre-records) and source-style cues (Shocklee, period songs) share the same 1970s soul and jazz vocabulary. The score can then pass into and out of the diegetic world without a clash. For a game set in a specific era or place, the composer can write "fake source" cues in the same idiom so that radios and environmental music become part of the score.
- **Tension from layered ostinati.** The *Body of Lies* model (constant percussion plus brooding sustained strings, with a regional solo instrument) is a cheap and effective layered system for stealth or investigation gameplay. Percussion density can scale with threat while the strings hold.

### Gaps
- No source describes specific tension cues in *American Gangster* beyond the general style.
- No Streitenfeld quote about either score was retrieved (the Cinemablend interview exists but was not read).
- No details on how Ridley Scott placed or mixed source songs against score in either film.

## Characteristic techniques, the composer's own statements and critical reception (cross-score synthesis, including Poltergeist 2015)

### Takeaway
Across his work, Streitenfeld puts sound-making ahead of melody. He builds every sound himself without sample libraries, transforms acoustic recordings (reversal, processing), uses solo colour instruments (saz, hurdy-gurdy, pipes), and keeps thematic material spare. Critics fairly consistently say his scores work in the film but disappoint as albums. *Poltergeist* (2015) continues the texture-first approach, with "creaky sound patterns" and a "twisted timbre".

### Cited Findings
**Poltergeist (2015)**
- Sony Classical press release: "creaky sound patterns" and an overall "twisted timbre". — [Sony Classical press release](https://sony.mediaroom.com/2015-05-07-Sony-Classical-Releases-Poltergeist-Soundtrack)
- Screen Anarchy: a varied palette of woodwinds, strings and percussion that avoids repetitive rhythms. — [Screen Anarchy](https://screenanarchy.com/2015/05/now-playing-poltergeist-ost-music-to-fight-ghosts.html)
- maintitles.net: apart from the opening cue and one rhythmic shift, little of it registers as music rather than mood and atmosphere. — [maintitles.net](https://maintitles.net/reviews/poltergeist-2015/)
- Kitley's Krypt: decent but nothing grabs you. Criticises heavy loud, thundering hits. Praises the opening theme and the nursery-style touches. — [Kitley's Krypt](https://kitleyskrypt.com/tag/soundracks/)
- Variety (2015) profiled him after he had finished mixing *Poltergeist*, focusing on his search for new sounds. — [Variety](https://variety.com/2015/music/features/composer-marc-streitenfeld-is-arduous-in-his-search-for-the-lost-chord-1201501614/)

**The composer's own statements (collected)**
- "I make all the sounds you hear in my scores." No sample libraries. A lot of time spent creating textures and percussive sounds. — [Variety 2015](https://variety.com/2015/music/features/composer-marc-streitenfeld-is-arduous-in-his-search-for-the-lost-chord-1201501614/)
- On reversal: "the same melody, but with a backwards sounding orchestra which gives it a kind of unusual, unsettling sound". — [Static Mass](http://staticmass.net/exclusive-interviews/marc-streitenfeld-prometheus-composer/)
- Process: script first, sometimes set visits, then rough cuts. — [GRAMMY.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/video-marc-streitenfeld-is-settling-the-score/)

**The critical pattern**
- *The Grey*: intelligent motific development, weak listenability ([Filmtracks](https://www.filmtracks.com/titles/grey.html)). Effective in the film, not engrossing on album ([Movie-Wave](https://www.movie-wave.net/the-grey/)).
- *Robin Hood*: underwhelming without images ([Filmtracks](https://www.filmtracks.com/titles/robin_hood10.html)).
- *Body of Lies*: loses its way as a standalone listen ([Movie Music UK](https://moviemusicuk.us/2008/10/10/body-of-lies-marc-streitenfeld/)).
- *Prometheus*: sound design intrudes, and the themes do not cohere ([Filmtracks](https://www.filmtracks.com/titles/prometheus.html)).
- *Poltergeist*: mostly mood rather than music ([maintitles.net](https://maintitles.net/reviews/poltergeist-2015/)).

### Inferences
Each item below is an inference built from the cited findings above, not a sourced claim.

1. **Make your own sound palette.** Record acoustic players or objects, then transform them (reverse, process). This is a direct, documented model.
2. **Pre-reverse the writing.** To make a melody "uncanny but recognisable", notate it backwards, record it, then reverse it. It works best on breath and woodwind sources, and players can be asked to shape phrasing so the reversed dynamics feel deliberate.
3. **Keep themes few and the texture bank large.** One or two themes, reserved for key moments (as with the Grey's two themes or Prometheus's "Life"), carried by extensive textural underscore. This suits adaptive game audio, where ambient layers loop and themes are triggered.
4. **Let silence and ambience score the world.** As in *The Grey*, environmental sound can carry long stretches. Music enters for emotion or memory.
5. **Use one solo colour instrument for identity.** Saz (*Body of Lies*), hurdy-gurdy, uilleann pipes or lute (*Robin Hood*), muted piano or solo strings (*The Grey*): a single timbre anchors place and character.
6. **Blur score and sound design.** Critics count it against him on album; in a game it is a strength.
7. **The warning.** Critics repeatedly say his scores lack memorable thematic identity on album. A game composer borrowing his texture-first method should consciously protect at least one strong theme, which is the job "Life" did in *Prometheus*.
