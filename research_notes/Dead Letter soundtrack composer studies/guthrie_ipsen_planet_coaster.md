## 1. How did Guthrie and Ipsen collaborate (division of labour, process, tools)?

### Takeaway
Guthrie and Ipsen are credited jointly and equally: "All music by Jim Guthrie and JJ Ipsen… Performed, recorded and mixed by Jim Guthrie and JJ Ipsen". They are two Ontario-based multi-instrumentalists who self-produced the score rather than working through an orchestra/score-production pipeline. No source found breaks down who wrote which cue. The hire started with a tiny lo-fi demo, just guitar and whistling. Ipsen later became the lead composer of the franchise (Planet Coaster 2).

### Inferences
- The equal "performed, recorded and mixed" credit, the small list of extra players and an indie mastering engineer (Andy Magoffin, House of Miracles in London, Ont.) suggest a **home-studio band record**: the two composers play most parts themselves, with a few guests for brass and strings. They did not use an orchestra. The intimacy of the sound follows from that.
- Guthrie in Guelph and Ipsen in Woodstock live about an hour apart, so the work was probably done by trading files plus some in-person sessions. This is unconfirmed.
- Ipsen's continuity into Planet Zoo, Planet Coaster 2 and Planet Zoo 2 means he probably shaped much of the brand's sound and is the person to study for the franchise's later in-game/adaptive music.
- The other composers (Pena, de Man, Shaw, Aux Verba) probably wrote much of the themed park-speaker library (Pirate, Western, Sci-Fi and so on). The Guthrie/Ipsen material probably carries the identity: main theme, menus, the album. Treat this as inference.
- **Lesson for a new score:** the pitch was a 30-second guitar-and-whistle demo. A tiny, very characterful sketch can sell a tone faster than a polished mockup.

## 2. Instrumentation and production (main theme, folk/acoustic/electronic/orchestral blend, retro elements, mixing aesthetic)

### Takeaway
The score is band-based indie folk-pop/rock rather than orchestral: acoustic guitars, piano, "massive" group vocals, brass, strings, handclaps and whistling. It is self-recorded and self-mixed, with a sunny, human, slightly lo-fi warmth. A companion ambient album, *Weightless*, made by in-house remixers, takes the same material into drone and texture.

### Cited Findings
- Official product description: "a dynamic cross section of acoustic guitars, massive vocals, brass, strings, handclaps, ...even whistlin'." — [Black Screen Records vinyl listing](https://blackscreenrecords.com/products/planet-coaster-original-soundtrack-by-jim-guthrie-jj-ipsen); [iam8bit 2xLP](https://www.iam8bit.com/products/planet-coaster-soundtrack-2xlp)
- Exclaim!: the 16-track album promises "a persistent upbeat and positive vibe". First single "Aspect Imaginarium" "makes use of layered guitars, piano and a horn section." — [Exclaim!](https://exclaim.ca/music/article/jim_guthrie_and_jj_ipsen_team_up_to_score_planet_coaster_videogame)
- Genre descriptors from critics: "cheery folk-pop and upbeat shuffles"; "euphoric indie pop rock mixed with theme park tropes" / "a catchy blend of indie pop and theme park tropes" (Higher Plain Music); "folky orchestral fusion" (Sound Architect); "vibrant, buoyant and at times introspective and emotional" (CBC); "a dynamic and moody album… a melancholy journey" (VGM WAX). — [Higher Plain Music review](https://higherplainmusic.com/2016/11/17/jim-guthrie-jj-ispen-you-me-and-gravity-the-music-of-planet-coaster-review/); [The Sound Architect: Planet Coaster game audio review](https://www.thesoundarchitect.co.uk/planet-coaster-game-audio-review/); [CBC Music](https://www.cbcmusic.ca/first-plays/46/jim-guthrie-jj-ipsen-you-me-gravity); [VGM WAX review](https://www.vgmwax.com/reviews/2018/10/17/planet-coaster)
- "Theories of an Eager Heart": "a breezy acoustic guitar led mid tempo rock track", whose "tick-tock rhythm" evokes building paths and queue lines (Higher Plain Music, paraphrased in excerpt). — [Higher Plain Music](https://higherplainmusic.com/2016/11/17/jim-guthrie-jj-ispen-you-me-and-gravity-the-music-of-planet-coaster-review/)
- Guthrie on fitting music to the game: he describes aiming for "the most musical version of non-musical ambience" and says "it would be ridiculous for me to put a really fat beat in this game." (excerpt-level) — [Bandcamp Daily](https://daily.bandcamp.com/high-scores/jim-guthrie-video-game-soundtrack)
- *Weightless: The Ambient Music of Planet Coaster* (11 April 2017): 16 tracks, about 28 minutes. Its tracks "appear in game as ambient versions of the official soundtrack songs, written by Jim Guthrie and JJ Ipsen". Remixers credited are Frontier staff Stephen Hollis, Ross Stack, Duncan Mackinnon, Jim Croft and Matthew Florianz. A "You, Me & Gravity (Jim Croft Mix)" exists on streaming services. — [VGMdb: Weightless](https://vgmdb.net/album/71069); [Apple Music: Weightless](https://music.apple.com/us/album/weightless-the-ambient-music-of-planet-coaster/1677617665); [Spotify: Jim Croft Mix](https://open.spotify.com/track/63wD5uhaK54EdO6WlirhlM)

One Discogs user notes vinyl surface noise on the quieter tracks, which shows that the album has quiet, sparse passages. — [VGMdb](https://vgmdb.net/album/63027); [VGM WAX review](https://www.vgmwax.com/reviews/2018/10/17/planet-coaster); [Discogs master](https://www.discogs.com/master/1209610-Jim-Guthrie-JJ-Ipsen-You-Me-Gravity)
- Guthrie's broader timbral habits: he layers near-identical sounds to thicken tone, and records long tape drones that he fades in and out. — [Native Instruments blog](https://blog.native-instruments.com/jim-guthrie-from-guitar-music-to-scoring-games/) (excerpt-level)

### Inferences
- **Palette as identity.** The defining sounds are performed and human: whistling, handclaps, gang vocals, strummed acoustic guitar. These are "people noises", and they map onto a park full of people. A composer can do the same by choosing instruments that stand for the game's social world, not just its genre.
- **Two-tier production.** A "foreground" song-form album sits beside an "ambient" derivative. Frontier's sound designers made the ambient versions from the same material, which gives a cheap, coherent way to get low-intensity in-game beds from your hero cues: stretch, filter, pad and drone them.
- No source documents analog synths or chiptune in Planet Coaster. The "retro" quality is more likely folk-pop and organ/fairground colour, as the Vintage pack's "Vintage Organ" pieces suggest (see Q3). Guthrie's chip/retro side belongs to Sword & Sworcery.
- Guthrie's "no fat beat" remark suggests a deliberate restraint on low-end and kick-driven grooves so the music sits under a dense park mix. That reading is inferred from the quote.

- No technical write-up was found on mics, guitars, synths, DAW, tempo or keys of specific tracks, or mix decisions such as reverb and stereo choices.
- No published analysis of the main theme's melody (intervals, contour, rhythm) was found.

## 3. Melody and theme: the main theme and its variations across menus, park and DLC; recurring motifs

### Takeaway
Only limited written analysis exists. What can be verified: the album opens with "The Light In Us All" and the title track "You, Me & Gravity" serves as the main/theme track. The DLC and the ambient album recycle and re-dress the main material. The Vintage pack's "Lights in Us Then" is evidently a period re-voicing of the opener, and the Ghostbusters pack re-arranges a licensed theme in several styles. That points to **theme-and-reorchestration** as the franchise's method for DLC.

### Cited Findings
- Verified track titles on *You, Me & Gravity* (16 tracks; the full list was not retrievable): opener "The Light In Us All" (Higher Plain renders it "The Light In All Us"); "Theories Of An Eager Heart" (about 4:06–4:07); "Sink Cat" (3:22); "Aspect Imaginarium" (4:29, first single); title track "You, Me & Gravity" (about 5:23); closer "And So We See The Truth & The Beauty" (about 4:50). — [VGMdb](https://vgmdb.net/album/63027); [Planet Coaster Wiki album page](https://planetcoaster.fandom.com/wiki/You,_Me_%26_Gravity:_The_Music_of_Planet_Coaster); [Exclaim!](https://exclaim.ca/music/article/jim_guthrie_and_jj_ipsen_team_up_to_score_planet_coaster_videogame)

Repeat](https://jimguthrie.bandcamp.com/track/dream-build-repeat-2); [Bandcamp: Trust](https://jimguthrie.bandcamp.com/track/trust)
- Critics single out the opener as a standout (Higher Plain Music), and "Aspect Imaginarium" and "Sink Cat" as highlights (CBC). — [Higher Plain Music](https://higherplainmusic.com/2016/11/17/jim-guthrie-jj-ispen-you-me-and-gravity-the-music-of-planet-coaster-review/); [CBC Music](https://www.cbcmusic.ca/first-plays/46/jim-guthrie-jj-ipsen-you-me-gravity)

### Inferences
- "The Light In Us All" and the Vintage-pack "Lights in Us Then" share a title, so the Vintage DLC most likely re-voices the opener's theme in an old-fashioned, organ or band-organ style. That is the clearest example of the main theme varied by DLC. The title match supports the inference, but no source confirms the music.
- The franchise varies its themes along three axes: (a) **texture** (ambient remixes for in-game beds), (b) **period/genre costume** (Vintage organ, the Ghostbusters theme as orchestral or acoustic), and (c) **brand title formula** ("You, Me & ___"). For a new game: write a few strong melodic cores, then plan "costume changes" (instrumentation, era, tempo) for areas and DLC instead of new themes each time.
- Guthrie's whistled demo suggests the main melodic identity is **whistle-able**: simple, singable and stepwise-plus-leaps, like a folk tune. This is consistent with the "whistlin'" in the official description, but no source analyses the theme's intervals.

## 5. Emotional tone: whimsy, nostalgia, wonder, warmth, and how it is achieved

### Takeaway
The brief was explicitly "warm, friendly, honest and authentic". The score gets there through human-performed, imperfect, communal sounds (whistling, handclaps, group vocals, acoustic guitar), a persistent positive mood, and restraint (no "fat beats"), with melancholy undertones that add emotional depth and nostalgia.

### Cited Findings
- Brief: "warm, friendly, honest and authentic audio experience" — [Planet Coaster Wiki](https://planetcoaster.fandom.com/wiki/Music_of_Planet_Coaster) (secondary source)
- "A persistent upbeat and positive vibe" — [Exclaim!](https://exclaim.ca/music/article/jim_guthrie_and_jj_ipsen_team_up_to_score_planet_coaster_videogame)
- "Euphoric indie pop rock mixed with theme park tropes" — [Higher Plain Music](https://higherplainmusic.com/2016/11/17/jim-guthrie-jj-ispen-you-me-and-gravity-the-music-of-planet-coaster-review/)
- "Bright and emotional… at times introspective" — [CBC Music](https://www.cbcmusic.ca/first-plays/46/jim-guthrie-jj-ipsen-you-me-gravity)
- "Melancholy journey" — [VGM WAX](https://www.vgmwax.com/reviews/2018/10/17/planet-coaster)
- Florianz's team began pre-production by sharing their own experiences of coaster parks and fairgrounds. They wanted music, mechanical sounds and screams, with special weight on the human "life" of the park. — [MCV/DEVELOP interview](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/interview-designing-the-audio-for-planet-coaster/) (excerpt-level)
- The track titles carry the sentiment: "The Light In Us All", "Theories Of An Eager Heart", "And So We See The Truth & The Beauty", "You, Me & Gravity". — [VGMdb](https://vgmdb.net/album/63027)

### Inferences
- **How the warmth is built:** (1) organic, "played by people in a room" instruments; (2) small imperfections such as whistling and claps that signal amateur joy rather than corporate polish; (3) earnest, almost diaristic titles that frame a management sim as being about people; (4) melancholy undertones so the positivity reads as heartfelt rather than saccharine.
- **Nostalgia** probably comes from the fairground and organ tropes in the Vintage material plus the folk-pop idiom, rather than from chiptune.
- Transferable principle: write the emotional brief in human adjectives ("honest", "friendly"), then pick instruments and performance styles that *sound* like those adjectives.

## 6. Implementation: menu vs in-game music, coexisting with a dense park soundscape, adaptive/looping design

### Takeaway
Frontier's audio rules were "dynamic, adaptive and interactive, and diegetic" where possible. Music in the park is mainly diegetic, placed through park speakers, rides and themed scenery, while the main soundtrack and its ambient versions supply non-diegetic beds. The ambient album is evidence of a deliberate low-intensity layer built from the hero material so music can sit under crowds and coasters. Detailed system documentation was not accessible.

### Cited Findings
- Design rules from a Frontier interview: the soundscape had to be "dynamic, adaptive and interactive, and diegetic unless those rules couldn't apply". "The soundscape (music, audio) had to be informed by the park build." — [MCV/DEVELOP](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/interview-designing-the-audio-for-planet-coaster/), via [Planet Coaster Wiki](https://planetcoaster.fandom.com/wiki/Music_of_Planet_Coaster) (excerpt-level)
- Fan wiki: the music is "dynamic and adaptive to the player's actions, generated in real time". Treat this as secondary and loosely worded. — [Planet Coaster Wiki](https://planetcoaster.fandom.com/wiki/Music_of_Planet_Coaster)
- The *Weightless* tracks "appear in game as ambient versions of the official soundtrack songs", remixed by Frontier audio staff. — [VGMdb: Weightless](https://vgmdb.net/album/71069)
- Placed and diegetic music: Park Speaker music is organised by theme (Pirate, Fairytale, Sci-Fi, Western, Vanilla, Holiday, Ghostbusters, Vintage) and sits under the scenery and special-effects categories. Players can assign music (including custom songs) to rides and loudspeakers, and one guide says custom music "doesn't affect the mood of the guests". — [Planet Coaster Wiki: Park Speaker – Music](https://planetcoaster.fandom.com/wiki/Park_Speaker_-_Music); [DTG Reviews: Add your own music](https://www.dtgre.com/2016/11/add-your-own-music-to-planet-coaster.html); [YouTube Sci-Fi speaker track](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S_EaJm91HAc)

- Guthrie's own framing: "the most musical version of non-musical ambience"; no "really fat beat". (excerpt-level) — [Bandcamp Daily](https://daily.bandcamp.com/high-scores/jim-guthrie-video-game-soundtrack)

### Inferences
- **Layered model:** (1) a sparse, soft non-diegetic bed built from ambient versions of the themes; (2) diegetic, spatialised music from speakers and rides, themed by area and placed by the player; (3) a heavy SFX and crowd layer. The full-band songs probably play on menus and in trailers, and with fuller intensity only where the bed would not fight the SFX. This is inferred, not documented.
- **Avoiding clutter:** few drums or sub-bass, folk instruments in the mid-range, and ambient versions in-game, so a busy mix of more than 20 coaster layers plus crowds stays readable.
- **Player-authored music placement** (speakers, ride audio, custom songs) turns music into a building material. That is a design idea worth borrowing for any game with spatial creativity.

### Gaps
- Not confirmed: the middleware (Wwise or Frontier's in-house tech), how music reacts to time of day or park state, menu-music logic, crossfade and loop rules, and whether music reacts to the camera's zoom level. The MCV and Designing Sound pieces are the likely sources.

## 7. Composers' statements, reception and awards; related work (Sword & Sworcery, Below, Indie Game: The Movie; Planet Coaster 2)

### Takeaway
Reception was warm in indie and VGM press (CBC, Exclaim!, Higher Plain Music, VGM WAX), and the album got a deluxe iam8bit vinyl. No awards for the score were verified. Guthrie's related work shows two approaches: Sword & Sworcery, a game built on his music, and Below, his music built on the game. Ipsen carried the franchise forward.

### Inferences
- Guthrie's Below drone vocabulary and Sword & Sworcery's lo-fi sequencing are a different expressive mode from Planet Coaster's bright folk-pop. His toolkit carries across (layering, tape drones, human imperfection), and that matches how the *Weightless* ambient layer relates to the songs.
- The "music-first vs game-first" framing from Capybara is a useful planning question for any new score: decide whether the game is built around the music or the music around the game. Planet Coaster sits in between, with a song-form album plus derived ambient beds for a systems-driven game.
