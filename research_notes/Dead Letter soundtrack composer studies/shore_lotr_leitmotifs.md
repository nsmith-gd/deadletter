## 1. Catalogue of major leitmotifs and their families: sound and meaning

### Takeaway
Shore organised more than 50 leitmotifs (some counts go to 90–100) into **culture families**. Each family has its own modal colour and its own "signature" instrument or choir: Hobbits (folk/Celtic, D major, tin whistle and fiddle), the Ring (three related themes: History, Seduction, Evil), Mordor (rhaita, low brass, Eastern-inflected), Isengard (5/4, taiko/anvils/chains), Elves (Lothlórien in an adapted maqam hijaz, Quenya choir), Dwarves (parallel fifths, male choir, Khuzdul), Rohan (Hardanger fiddle, Nordic), Gondor (brass, Realm of Gondor and Minas Tirith), Gollum (Pity theme and cimbalom "Menace") and Nature (Nature's Reclamation, boy soprano).

### Cited Findings
**Scale of the system / sources**
- Shore wrote "over 50 leitmotifs to represent the different characters, cultures and places." — [Wikipedia: Music of The Lord of the Rings film series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings_film_series). Other counts are higher: a forum post says "between 90-100", and another article says "over 100 distinct motifs". These are lower-quality sources, and the count depends on how motifs and sub-variants are split. — [search summary, LotR leitmotif catalogue query](https://forums.sim-football.com/showthread.php?tid=36985)
- Doug Adams' *The Music of The Lord of the Rings Films* (2010, ~401 pp. + CD, with notation) is the authorised reference. Its "Themes" and "Annotated Score" sections were previously released in condensed form as the Complete Recordings liner notes. — [Wikipedia: The Music of the Lord of the Rings Films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Music_of_the_Lord_of_the_Rings_Films); [backgroundscore.com interview with Adams](https://www.backgroundscore.com/2010/09/doug-adams-speaks-about-book-music-of.html)
- FotR Annotated Score (companion PDF): [elvish.org](https://www.elvish.org/gwaith/pdf/fotr_annotated_score_2.pdf). RotK Annotated Score: [tolkiendil.com](https://www.tolkiendil.com/_media/divertissements/adaptations/films/sda_3_livret_de_presentation_de_la_bo_en_anglais_.pdf)

**The Shire / Hobbits**
- The Shire theme ("Concerning Hobbits") is a **stepwise melody in D major**, played by strings, solo fiddle or tin whistle. — [Wikipedia: Concerning Hobbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concerning_Hobbits)
- Adams labels the folk setting a separate **Hobbiton** theme, "played by solo fiddle and various Celtic instruments in accompaniment". The suite opens with the Hobbit "outline" theme on solo tin whistle, and the whistle returns for the final Shire statement. — [search summary citing Concerning Hobbits / Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concerning_Hobbits); [classicalexburns.com](https://classicalexburns.com/2021/12/22/howard-shore-concerning-hobbits-folk-inspired/)
- Fan catalogue names a "Rural Setting of the Shire/Hobbit Theme" as a distinct setting. — [amagpiesnest.com](https://www.amagpiesnest.com/themes/THshire_rural.htm)
- The high D tin whistle is the instrument most associated with the soundtrack. — [McNeela Music blog](https://blog.mcneelamusic.com/lord-of-the-rings-tin-whistle/)

**The Fellowship**
- The Fellowship theme represents the group's combined strength and belief in each other. It is a calm theme as the hobbits leave the Shire. — [search summary: Ludwig-Van / soundoflife](https://www.soundoflife.com/blogs/people/howard-shore-music-lord-of-the-rings)
- The Fellowship theme's opening **"down-and-back" contour** is reused as the basis of the "Heroics of Aragorn" and "Gandalf the White" material, and it closes the Gondor theme in its ascending form. — [RotK Annotated Score (tolkiendil.com)](https://www.tolkiendil.com/_media/divertissements/adaptations/films/sda_3_livret_de_presentation_de_la_bo_en_anglais_.pdf)
- A fan analysis says the Fellowship theme moves in six beats per bar, and that a shift to five beats marks the Uruk-hai (Isengard) theme. — [lotrscrapbook musical analysis](http://www.lotrscrapbook.bookloaf.net/ref/music/contents/track13.html) *(fan source)*

**The Ring (three themes)**
- **History of the Ring**: sounds when the Ring passes between holders. Shore: "Now that Frodo's taken the Ring you hear the History of the Ring theme. He's physically touched it and is holding it in his hand, so it's passed from Bilbo to Frodo." — [FotR Annotated Score](https://www.elvish.org/gwaith/pdf/fotr_annotated_score_2.pdf)
- History of the Ring has a rise-and-fall shape meant to "anthropomorphize the ring". — [Macksey Journal article, "Even Darkness Must Pass"](https://mackseyjournal.scholasticahq.com/api/v1/articles/28026-even-darkness-must-pass-an-ethical-commentary-and-musical-analysis-of-_the-lord-of-the-rings_-score.pdf)
- Filmtracks praises "the use of a singular, creaky old violin to depict the origins of the ring's history theme". — [Filmtracks: RotK review](https://www.filmtracks.com/titles/lord_rings03.html)
- **Seduction of the Ring**: sung by boys' voices, "a suggestion of false purity that the Ring promises its victims" ([Macksey Journal](https://mackseyjournal.scholasticahq.com/api/v1/articles/28026-even-darkness-must-pass-an-ethical-commentary-and-musical-analysis-of-_the-lord-of-the-rings_-score.pdf)). Concert notes describe it as "pure, clean choral tones … punctuated by rumbling bass drum strokes", representing the Ring's "overwhelming allure". — [LotR in Concert: FotR](https://www.lordoftheringsinconcert.com/fotr)
- **Evil of the Ring** (also called the Sauron / Mordor theme): "an impervious snarl, limited to only four or five pitches, and unwavering in its focus", with "Eastern tinted harmonic inflections" suggesting a past age. — [search summary of Annotated Score / LotR in Concert](https://www.lordoftheringsinconcert.com/rotk)
- The FotR Prologue lists Mordor's **"Skip Beat"** accompaniment and the **"Descending Third"** accompaniment alongside the Sauron/Evil of the Ring theme. — [FotR Annotated Score](https://www.elvish.org/gwaith/pdf/fotr_annotated_score_2.pdf)

**Mordor**
- The **rhaita** (a North African double-reed, oboe-like instrument) represents the cultures of Mordor. Shore creates a semi-canon by having the rhaita enter just after the orchestral winds in the Evil of the Ring theme. — [search summary: LotR fandom soundtrack page / Annotated Score](https://lotr.fandom.com/wiki/Soundtracks_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings_film_trilogy)
- Mordor music relies on "low strings and the deepest registers of brass", morbid in contrast to the warm Shire material. — [search summary, Ring-theme analysis query](https://www.filmtracks.com/comments/titles/lord_rings01/index.cgi?read=1429)

**Isengard**
- The Isengard theme is in **5/4**, tied to Saruman's industrialism. — [TV Tropes: Howard Shore](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Music/HowardShore)
- Isengard music uses "Japanese Taiko drums, blacksmith anvils and metal chains" with six heavy, rhythmic notes. Low brass, 5/4 and metal percussion emphasise industrialism. — [search summary, LotR fandom](https://lotr.fandom.com/wiki/Soundtracks_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings_film_trilogy); [AUB Outlook article](https://sites.aub.edu.lb/outlook/?p=5890)

**Elves: Lothlórien and Rivendell**
- Adams: the Lothlórien music uses "an Arabic mode not unlike the Western Phrygian mode, though with an augmented second between its second and third scale degrees" (p. 54 of his book, per secondary citation). It is "the most Eastern and exotic of all the Elves' music". — [search summary citing Adams via Galadriel article / blog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galadriel); [lotsoflux blog: "LOTR, to the tune of Arabia"](https://lotsoflux.wordpress.com/2010/10/05/lotr-prologue-in-hijaz/)
- Per an Adams excerpt quoted on a blog, the augmented second is **not** meant to evoke "otherness". It creates "a sense of age that speaks of Middle-earth's ancient eras". — [lotsoflux blog](https://lotsoflux.wordpress.com/2010/10/05/lotr-prologue-in-hijaz/)
- The Galadriel/Lothlórien theme is described as an adapted **maqam hijaz** with droning eastern instruments, including a monochord. — [Wikipedia: Galadriel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galadriel)
- Shore: "Lothlórien is more exotic." — [search summary citing Adams/Shore](https://www.amagpiesnest.com/themes/THelves_lothlorien.htm)
- Collider: Shore used a **sarangi, ney flute and monochord**, with a chant in **Quenya**. — [Collider: "Composer Howard Shore: The True King"](https://collider.com/composer-howard-shore-the-lord-of-the-rings/)
- Rivendell theme: no specific technical description surfaced (see Gaps).

**Dwarves / Moria**
- The basic Dwarven material is "a series of parallel fifths set for a male choir", heard through Moria. — [LotR fandom: Music of the Middle-earth film series](https://lotr.fandom.com/wiki/Music_of_the_Middle-earth_film_series)
- Lyrics are by Philippa Boyens, translated into (neo-)Khuzdul by David Salo. — [elvish.org Soundtrack Analysis](https://www.elvish.org/gwaith/movie_soundtrack_fotr.htm); [Wikipedia: Music of LotR film series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings_film_series)
- The trilogy's sung languages include English, Old English, Sindarin, Quenya, Khuzdul, Black Speech and Adûnaic. A forum post associates Adûnaic with the Nazgûl. — [Wikipedia: Music of LotR film series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings_film_series); [Filmtracks forum](https://www.filmtracks.com/comments/titles/lord_rings02/index.cgi?read=743&expand=1) *(forum)*

**Rohan**
- The Rohan theme is the main new theme of *The Two Towers*, with a "marked Nordic feeling". It is played on the **Hardanger fiddle** (Norway's national instrument, with sympathetic strings under the fingerboard), performed by Dermot Crehan. — [search summary: TheOneRing.net "the Rohan theme and the fiddle"](https://theonering.net/torwp/2003/01/08/22545-the-rohan-theme-and-the-fiddle)
- Reviewers call it a "noble fanfare", often on heroic trumpets or the Hardanger, and "vaguely Celtic". The first full statements come in "Edoras", after "worn snippets" of the Fellowship and Rohan themes. — [Filmtracks: TTT](https://www.filmtracks.com/titles/lord_rings02.html); [Movie Music UK: TTT](https://moviemusicuk.us/2002/12/20/the-lord-of-the-rings-the-two-towers-howard-shore/)

**Gondor**
- **Realm of Gondor** is the de facto central theme of RotK. The French horn intones its opening at the title card. — [RotK Annotated Score](https://www.tolkiendil.com/_media/divertissements/adaptations/films/sda_3_livret_de_presentation_de_la_bo_en_anglais_.pdf)
- A basic version is first established at the Council of Elrond in FotR. — [Principal leitmotifs mirror blog](http://singwikicode.blogspot.com/2011/01/principal-leitmotifs-music-of-lord-of.html) *(Wikipedia mirror)*
- Two brass-led Gondor themes: the "leaping" Realm of Gondor and the "more introspective" Minas Tirith theme. — [LotR in Concert: RotK](https://www.lordoftheringsinconcert.com/rotk)

**Gollum**
- **Pity of Gollum (Sméagol's theme)**: "enfolding triads" for his wretched state ([melson.nl](https://www.melson.nl/lotr/index.php@view=theme&id=12.html)). Annotated Score: "wretched and vile to be sure, but in the end, he's a victim of the Ring". Its greatest dramatic weight comes in the Moria sequence. — [FotR Annotated Score](https://www.elvish.org/gwaith/pdf/fotr_annotated_score_2.pdf)
- Its opening harmonies are **B♭ minor and F♯ minor**, which "swell and ebb" in the Dead Marshes. — [RotK/TTT Annotated Score via search summary](https://www.tolkiendil.com/_media/divertissements/adaptations/films/sda_3_livret_de_presentation_de_la_bo_en_anglais_.pdf)
- **Gollum's Menace** uses the **cimbalom** ("twitchy chromatic figures") to represent "the side of him that is not piteous". — [amagpiesnest Pity of Gollum](https://www.amagpiesnest.com/themes/THgollum_pity.htm); [Movie Music UK: TTT](https://moviemusicuk.us/2002/12/20/the-lord-of-the-rings-the-two-towers-howard-shore/)

**Nature / Ents**
- **Nature's Reclamation** is the main nature theme, "usually carried by boy soprano voice or violins". It is used for the Moth and the Last March of the Ents, and has been called a theme for the Free Peoples reclaiming their true nature. — [amagpiesnest: Nature's Reclamation](https://www.amagpiesnest.com/themes/THnature.htm); [LotR fandom](https://lotr.fandom.com/wiki/Music_of_the_Middle-earth_film_series)
- Ent music leans on wooden instruments: "slitted log drums" and "dragging notes" suggest slow movement and age. — [Movie Music UK / Filmtracks TTT reviews](https://www.filmtracks.com/titles/lord_rings02.html)

### Inferences
- **Design principle: family = mode + timbre + rhythm, and theme = contour.** Each culture is identified before a melody even starts. Examples: Isengard by metre (5/4) plus metallic percussion, Dwarves by parallel fifths plus male choir, Rohan by one solo folk instrument, Lothlórien by a single characteristic interval (the augmented 2nd). A puzzle game can borrow this. Give each region or faction a *non-melodic* fingerprint (metre, interval, timbre) so that ambient/loop music identifies the place even when no theme is playing.
- The Ring is split into **three themes for three aspects** of one object (its past and transfer, its allure, its malevolence). This is a useful model for a game's central MacGuffin or mystery. Give it a "history" motif for revelations, a "lure" motif for when the player is tempted, and a "threat" motif for consequences.
- Adams' point that the augmented second signals *age* rather than *otherness* is worth adopting. Exotic intervals can encode time depth (ancient vs. modern layers of a setting) rather than geography.
- *Unverified background recollection, not sourced in this session:* Adams' book also describes a "Mordor" theme family that shares Arabic/Phrygian colouring with Lothlórien, so that the Eastern inflection marks the age of both the Elves and Sauron. He also gives a separate "Rivendell" theme with arpeggiated, choral Elvish writing. Verify before use.

### Gaps
- Exact intervals or notation for Fellowship, Gondor, Rohan, Rivendell and Evil of the Ring could not be confirmed, because the Annotated Score PDFs and Wikipedia's leitmotif table were unreachable.
- The "Shire is pentatonic" claim was **not** confirmed by any source found. The confirmed description is "stepwise melody in D major". A tin-whistle tutorial gives "D whistle in the key of G", which conflicts with D major, so the key may vary by setting.
- Rhythm of the Mordor "Skip Beat" and the mode of Evil of the Ring: not found.
- An Adûnaic choir specifically for Mordor or the Nazgûl was asserted only by a forum post.

## 2. Transformation of themes to track the story

### Takeaway
Shore's transformations are tied to dramatic state. The Fellowship theme is **assembled from fragments** as members join, reaches full form at Rivendell, and is **shattered into shards** after Moria and the Breaking. The Gondor theme carries an unresolved "Decline" ending that is replaced by an "Ascension" ending when Aragorn claims his role. Gollum's music shifts from Pity to Menace as his good side loses. In RotK the three Ring themes increasingly interlock contrapuntally.

### Cited Findings
- **Fellowship assembled by fragments.** "Shore's theme for the Fellowship of the Ring is assembled by fragments throughout the first film and scattered into shards thereafter." It is not complete until Rivendell. — [LotR in Concert: FotR](https://www.lordoftheringsinconcert.com/fotr)
- Shore (Annotated Score), on an incomplete statement before Rivendell: "it's not completely assembled, but it's getting closer because now Strider has joined them." — [FotR Annotated Score](https://www.elvish.org/gwaith/pdf/fotr_annotated_score_2.pdf)
- The theme plays "rather fragmented, slowly increasing in power, until the group fully forms". — [Zanobard Reviews](https://zanobardreviews.com/2026/01/09/the-lord-of-the-rings-the-fellowship-of-the-ring-soundtrack-review/)
- After the Breaking, the theme is "disassembled and never heard in the same heroic vibe again" (one reviewer, [Renovatio Records](http://renovatiorecords.blogspot.com/2023/12/the-lord-of-rings-fellowship-of-ring.html)). Another analysis, citing Adams, says the theme returns in RotK at its "heroic zenith". — [Allegro Media analysis](https://allegromediamusic.wordpress.com/2018/05/19/lord-of-the-rings-music-analysis-part-1-the-fellowship-theme/). *These two sources partly conflict. The likely reconciliation is that the full heroic form is withheld in TTT and returns in RotK.*
- In TTT, "worn snippets" of the Fellowship theme open "Edoras". — [Movie Music UK: TTT](https://moviemusicuk.us/2002/12/20/the-lord-of-the-rings-the-two-towers-howard-shore/)
- "The Breaking of the Fellowship" combines the Hobbit theme (now "somber and pensive") with the Fellowship theme and sections of the Rivendell theme. — [unobtainium13 blog](https://unobtainium13.com/2011/06/24/song-of-the-day-the-breaking-of-the-fellowship-by-howard-shore/) *(fan blog)*
- **Gondor Decline → Ascension.** In RotK the Realm of Gondor theme is repeatedly left unresolved. The score asks "whether we are now seeing Gondor in Ascension or in Decline", then darkens again. — [RotK Annotated Score](https://www.tolkiendil.com/_media/divertissements/adaptations/films/sda_3_livret_de_presentation_de_la_bo_en_anglais_.pdf). "The Realm of Gondor (In Decline)" in TTT's Eyes of the White Tower cue — [melson.nl](https://www.melson.nl/lotr/index.php@view=theme&id=20.html)
- "When Aragorn's theme attaches itself to Gondor, it replaces the decline figure with its own ascending ending." The shift happens as Gandalf tells Aragorn he must come to Minas Tirith. The "Heroics of Aragorn" form the ascending coda of Gondor in Ascension. — [LotR fandom](https://lotr.fandom.com/wiki/Music_of_the_Middle-earth_film_series); [RotK Annotated Score](https://www.tolkiendil.com/_media/divertissements/adaptations/films/sda_3_livret_de_presentation_de_la_bo_en_anglais_.pdf); [leitmotif list mirror](http://singwikicode.blogspot.com/2011/01/principal-leitmotifs-music-of-lord-of.html)
- The Minas Tirith theme develops into the celebratory music of Aragorn's coronation. — [LotR fandom](https://lotr.fandom.com/wiki/Music_of_the_Middle-earth_film_series)
- **Gollum.** The Pity theme appears "very little" in RotK, where the cimbalom-based Menace represents him "throughout most of" the score. The two are fused at times, e.g. the Dead Marshes (Pity harmonies) and cimbalom "quivering" under Gollum's sobbing. — [search summary of Annotated Score / amagpiesnest](https://www.amagpiesnest.com/themes/THgollum_pity.htm)
- **Ring themes.** In TTT, the Déagol/Sméagol struggle fuses the Seduction and Evil themes. In RotK the three Ring themes "increasingly interact, intertwining contrapuntally or pulling at melodic contours to form new lines". — [search summary of RotK Annotated Score / LotR in Concert](https://www.lordoftheringsinconcert.com/rotk)
- **Nature's Reclamation** builds from "earlier hints" (e.g. the Moth in FotR) to full choral and orchestral force in "The Last March of the Ents". — [Filmtracks: TTT](https://www.filmtracks.com/titles/lord_rings02.html); [amagpiesnest](https://www.amagpiesnest.com/themes/THnature.htm)

### Inferences
- **Transformation techniques catalogue, for a puzzle game:**
  1. *Additive assembly*: the theme gains phrases as party members or clues are gathered (Fellowship). In a puzzle game this maps directly to collected pieces of a mystery.
  2. *Fragmentation into shards*: after loss, quote only the head motif, in thin orchestration (Fellowship after Moria).
  3. *Cadence substitution*: the same melody body with a different ending (Gondor Decline vs. Ascension). This is cheap and very readable, and it suits branching outcomes or a solved/unsolved state.
  4. *Contour borrowing across themes*: the Fellowship's down-and-back shape becomes Aragorn's ascending coda on Gondor. This joins character arcs by sharing an interval cell.
  5. *Theme balance shift*: Pity → Menace for Gollum. Two themes for one character, with their ratio tracking moral state.
  6. *Contrapuntal fusion*: Ring themes combined at climaxes.
  7. *Seeded early, paid off late*: hint a theme long before its full statement (the Moth → Ents march; Council of Elrond → Realm of Gondor).

### Gaps
- Specific Shire settings by cue (rural vs. pensive vs. hymn-like) could not be documented in detail. Only "Rural Setting" (fan catalogue) and "somber and pensive" in the Breaking of the Fellowship were found.
- The cue where the Fellowship theme reaches its RotK "heroic zenith" was not identified.

## 3. Thematic relationships via shared intervals or modes

### Takeaway
The documented relationships are: (a) the Fellowship's down-and-back opening cell recurs in Aragorn's and Gandalf the White's heroics and as Gondor's ascending coda; (b) Lothlórien's mode is described as Arabic/Phrygian with an augmented second that signals antiquity; (c) Evil of the Ring has a narrow 4–5 pitch range with "Eastern-tinted" harmony; (d) Pity of Gollum is built on remote minor triads (B♭m–F♯m). A university course page claims every important theme traces back to the main theme, but that claim is thinly supported.

### Cited Findings
- Fellowship down-and-back contour → Heroics of Aragorn, Gandalf the White, and the closing of Gondor in Ascension. — [RotK Annotated Score](https://www.tolkiendil.com/_media/divertissements/adaptations/films/sda_3_livret_de_presentation_de_la_bo_en_anglais_.pdf)
- Lothlórien: Arabic mode akin to Phrygian with an augmented second between degrees 2 and 3, signalling "age". — [lotsoflux (quoting Adams)](https://lotsoflux.wordpress.com/2010/10/05/lotr-prologue-in-hijaz/); [Wikipedia: Galadriel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galadriel)
- Evil of the Ring: 4–5 pitches with Eastern-tinted inflections suggesting a past age. — [LotR in Concert: RotK](https://www.lordoftheringsinconcert.com/rotk)
- A blog transposes the LotR Prologue into "hijaz", which suggests the Prologue material itself carries this colour. — [lotsoflux blog](https://lotsoflux.wordpress.com/2010/10/05/lotr-prologue-in-hijaz/)
- "Every single important musical theme may be traced back to the main theme" (course page claim). — [LibreTexts: Howard Shore's Score to Lord of the Rings](https://human.libretexts.org/Courses/Prince_Georges_Community_College/Music_Appreciation%3A_A_Topical_Approach_to_Music_Genre_and_Style/13%3A_Music_in_Film/13.04%3A_Howard_Shore's_Score_to_Lord_of_the_Rings) *(introductory course text; unverified)*
- Metre as a relationship marker: Fellowship in six beats, Uruk-hai/Isengard in five. — [lotrscrapbook](http://www.lotrscrapbook.bookloaf.net/ref/music/contents/track13.html); [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Music/HowardShore)

### Inferences
- **Shared modal colour can link the ancient good and the ancient evil.** The Elves (Lothlórien) and Sauron's material both carry an Eastern, augmented-second colour that Adams links to *age*. This suggests Shore links things by **era** rather than by moral alignment, which is a sophisticated idea a puzzle game can use (e.g. a "first civilisation" modal colour shared by benevolent and malevolent remnants). *The Mordor half of this needs verification.*
- **Metre contrast as a moral signal:** compound/triple flow for the Free Peoples vs. asymmetric 5/4 machinery for Isengard. A puzzle game could put mechanical antagonists in odd metres.
- *Unverified background recollection:* the Shire's "Irish/pentatonic" character is commonly asserted. The sourced fact is a folk-style, stepwise D-major melody with Celtic instrumentation. Pentatonicism is not confirmed here.

### Gaps
- The interval-level relationship between the Shire and Fellowship themes, or between Rohan and Gondor ("Men" themes), could not be confirmed.
- Adams' explicit discussion of theme families and shared cells (in the book's "Themes" chapter) was unreachable.

## 4. Orchestration identity: signature instruments, choirs, languages, soloists

### Takeaway
Each culture gets a **solo timbral signature** set against the shared symphonic core of the London Philharmonic and NZSO. These are tin whistle/fiddle (Shire), rhaita (Mordor), Hardanger fiddle (Rohan), cimbalom (Gollum), sarangi/ney/monochord (Lothlórien), taiko, anvils and chains (Isengard), log drums (Ents) and a male choir (Dwarves). Choirs sing texts by Philippa Boyens translated (largely by David Salo) into Tolkien's languages. The boy soprano carries purity, both genuine (Nature's Reclamation) and false (Seduction of the Ring).

### Cited Findings
- Tin whistle (Shire); Hardanger fiddle, played by Dermot Crehan (Rohan); rhaita (Mordor); cimbalom (Gollum's Menace). — sources as in §1: [McNeela](https://blog.mcneelamusic.com/lord-of-the-rings-tin-whistle/), [TheOneRing.net](https://theonering.net/torwp/2003/01/08/22545-the-rohan-theme-and-the-fiddle), [LotR fandom](https://lotr.fandom.com/wiki/Soundtracks_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings_film_trilogy), [amagpiesnest](https://www.amagpiesnest.com/themes/THgollum_pity.htm)
- Lothlórien: sarangi, ney flute, monochord, and a Quenya chant. — [Collider](https://collider.com/composer-howard-shore-the-lord-of-the-rings/)
- Isengard: taiko, blacksmith anvils, metal chains. — [LotR fandom](https://lotr.fandom.com/wiki/Soundtracks_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings_film_trilogy)
- Ents: slitted log drums and wood-related instruments. — [Movie Music UK / Filmtracks TTT](https://www.filmtracks.com/titles/lord_rings02.html)
- Dwarves: male choir in parallel fifths. Khuzdul text by Boyens and Salo. — [LotR fandom](https://lotr.fandom.com/wiki/Music_of_the_Middle-earth_film_series); [elvish.org](https://www.elvish.org/gwaith/movie_soundtrack_fotr.htm)
- Boy soprano Ben Del Maestro sings Nature's Reclamation in "The Last March of the Ents", with a boys' choir singing Entish lyrics. — [search summary: soundscapenotes / sonichits](https://www.soundscapenotes.com/single-post/2017/02/26/lord-of-the-rings-complete-recordings)
- Seduction of the Ring: boys' voices express "false purity". — [Macksey Journal](https://mackseyjournal.scholasticahq.com/api/v1/articles/28026-even-darkness-must-pass-an-ethical-commentary-and-musical-analysis-of-_the-lord-of-the-rings_-score.pdf)
- Bridge of Khazad-dûm performer credits include the London Oratory School Schola, London Voices and the NZSO. Fan sources call the low choir there "Polynesian" or "Maori", which is unresolved. — [Shazam](https://www.shazam.com/en-us/song/323714147/the-bridge-of-khazad-dc3bbm); [Film Music Central](https://filmmusiccentral.com/2016/08/11/the-fellowship-of-the-ring-the-bridge-of-khazad-dum-2001/)
- Sung languages: English, Old English, Sindarin, Quenya, Khuzdul, Black Speech, Adûnaic. — [Wikipedia: Music of LotR film series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_The_Lord_of_the_Rings_film_series)
- "Lament for Gandalf" (album track "Lothlórien") was written by Boyens and Shore and sung by Elizabeth Fraser. — [Wikipedia: FotR soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring_(soundtrack))

### Inferences
- **The same timbre can carry opposite meanings when context differs.** The boy soprano is used for both the Ring's false purity and nature's true renewal. This invites the audience to recognise the counterfeit. For a puzzle game, an "innocent" timbre that later proves to belong to the deceiver is a strong reveal device.
- **One folk soloist per culture** is economical. In a game with limited budget or live players, a single signature instrument per region does more identity work than full orchestration changes.
- Setting texts in invented or dead languages lets choirs carry semantic content (the Khuzdul text narrates Moria) without distracting the audience. A game can hide puzzle clues in sung lyrics in a constructed language, translated later as a reward. *This is my inference, not Shore's practice.*

- Full instrument list for the Shire (e.g. bodhrán, dulcimer) and Rivendell was not confirmed in this session.

## 5. Major emotional set pieces and how the music builds them

### Takeaway
Set pieces follow a consistent design. Long-seeded themes get their first full or most extreme statement at the dramatic peak, often carried by an unprecedented timbre (choir enters at the Balrog's naming; the boy soprano and full choir at the Ents' march). Statements stay deliberately unresolved until the payoff (the Beacons end on the Gondor Decline figure). The sources are much stronger on what is used than on bar-by-bar construction.

### Cited Findings
- **Lighting of the Beacons:** "With a lattice of rising figures, the brass steels upwards, emerging in a powerful, magniloquent statement of the Gondor theme", yet phrases still end with the **Gondor in Decline** figure. It is a fast reprise of the Gondor theme. — [Last.fm wiki: The Lighting of the Beacons](https://www.last.fm/music/Howard+Shore/_/The+Lighting+of+the+Beacons/+wiki); [Film Music Central](https://filmmusiccentral.com/2019/02/05/the-return-of-the-king-lighting-the-beacons-2003/)
- **Bridge of Khazad-dûm:** A minor, 6/8 (Hooktheory, a fan transcription site, gives i–iv–VI and ~164 BPM). The choir enters when Gandalf names the Balrog. The Dwarvish male-choir text is in Khuzdul. It builds from a melancholic opening to a choral and orchestral climax, and the full-length version is roughly 9 minutes against 5 on the album. — [Hooktheory](https://www.hooktheory.com/theorytab/view/howard-shore/bridge-of-khazad-dum); [Film Music Central](https://filmmusiccentral.com/2016/08/11/the-fellowship-of-the-ring-the-bridge-of-khazad-dum-2001/); [swashbuckler332 LiveJournal](https://swashbuckler332.livejournal.com/321215.html)
- The lament for Gandalf follows in Lothlórien (Elizabeth Fraser, adapted hijaz over drone-like open low strings). — [Wikipedia: FotR soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring_(soundtrack)); [Wikipedia: Galadriel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galadriel)
- **Grey Havens / "Into the West":** The instrumental material of "Into the West" "forms the theme of the Grey Havens" and is heard in "The Eagles" and "The Journey to the Grey Havens" before the full song plays over the credits. — [Wikipedia: Into the West (song)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Into_the_West_(song))
- **"You bow to no one" / coronation:** Fans say a long, sweeping Shire theme "sells the moment". The cue is "The Return of the King" (album track). — [ResetEra thread](https://www.resetera.com/threads/after-multiple-viewings-my-friends-you-bow-to-no-one-from-return-of-the-king-is-corny.69387/); [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/lord-of-the-rings-best-music-moments-ranked/) *(fan opinion, no technical analysis found)*. The Minas Tirith theme develops into the coronation's celebratory music. — [LotR fandom](https://lotr.fandom.com/wiki/Music_of_the_Middle-earth_film_series)
- **Last March of the Ents:** Nature's Reclamation reaches full choral and orchestral force with boy soprano Ben Del Maestro. — [Filmtracks TTT](https://www.filmtracks.com/titles/lord_rings02.html); [soundscapenotes](https://www.soundscapenotes.com/single-post/2017/02/26/lord-of-the-rings-complete-recordings)
- The RotK score is the most expansive of the three, with over four hours of finalised music. — [Wikipedia: RotK soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Return_of_the_King_(soundtrack))

### Inferences
- **Beacons template:** state the theme at full power but end it on the "decline" cadence, which delivers spectacle while withholding resolution. Save the "ascension" ending for when the protagonist actually takes up their role. In a puzzle game, play the main theme gloriously when a chapter's mechanism is solved, but end it on an unresolved cadence until the overarching mystery is solved.
- **Bridge template:** the timbral reveal (choir) is synchronised with a naming or identification beat. For a story puzzle game, the "aha" moment when a culprit or truth is named is the natural place for a new timbre's first entrance.
- **Grey Havens template:** an end-credits song's melody is planted instrumentally earlier, so the credits song feels like a recognised arrival rather than new material.
- **"You bow to no one" (inference from fan reports plus known structure):** the payoff of an epic story lands on the *smallest, most domestic* theme (the Shire) rather than the grandest. Returning to the humble home theme at the highest-status moment reframes what the story was about. This is a powerful closing tool for an intimate puzzle narrative.

- No technical, bar-level analysis of the coronation / "You bow to no one" cue, the Grey Havens cue, or the Beacons' modulation scheme was reachable. Adams' Annotated Score for RotK (tolkiendil PDF) contains this and should be read directly.

## 6. Restraint: avoiding overuse and withholding full statements

### Takeaway
The clearest documented restraint device is **withholding the complete Fellowship theme** until the group is complete at Rivendell, then fragmenting it. Gondor's theme is held in "Decline" form for most of its appearances, and the full ascending form comes only with Aragorn. Gollum's Pity theme is rationed to near-absence in RotK. No source found uses the word "restraint" as a Shore quote.

### Cited Findings
- Fellowship withheld until Rivendell, then "scattered into shards". — [LotR in Concert: FotR](https://www.lordoftheringsinconcert.com/fotr). Shore: "it's not completely assembled, but it's getting closer…" — [FotR Annotated Score](https://www.elvish.org/gwaith/pdf/fotr_annotated_score_2.pdf)
- Gondor is repeatedly left unresolved in RotK, and the ascending ending is reserved for Aragorn's acceptance. — [RotK Annotated Score](https://www.tolkiendil.com/_media/divertissements/adaptations/films/sda_3_livret_de_presentation_de_la_bo_en_anglais_.pdf); [LotR fandom](https://lotr.fandom.com/wiki/Music_of_the_Middle-earth_film_series)
- The Pity theme appears "very little" in RotK. — [amagpiesnest](https://www.amagpiesnest.com/themes/THgollum_pity.htm)
- The search found no source attributing a "withholding/restraint" philosophy to Shore in those words. — [search result note](https://www.ludwig-van.com/toronto/2021/12/17/feature-composer-howard-shore-looks-back-on-his-work-for-the-lord-of-the-rings/)
- The Realm of Gondor is introduced in basic form at the Council of Elrond (FotR) two films before its payoff. — [leitmotif list mirror](http://singwikicode.blogspot.com/2011/01/principal-leitmotifs-music-of-lord-of.html)

### Inferences
- Shore's restraint works through **form, not silence**. Themes are present but incomplete, or complete but with the wrong ending. This keeps them familiar while saving the "true" statement. For game loops, this suggests making looping area music use partial or unresolved theme forms by default, and triggering the complete form only at story beats. That avoids listener fatigue from repetition.
- **Rationing a character's sympathetic theme** as their arc darkens (Pity in RotK) makes its rare reappearances land harder.

### Gaps
- No direct Shore or Adams quote on a general policy of restraint was found.

## 7. Shore's own statements and reception (awards)

### Takeaway
Shore says he used themes "for clarity in storytelling" and saw the Sam/Frodo relationship as the heart of the story. The scores won Academy Awards for FotR (Best Original Score, 74th Academy Awards) and RotK (Best Original Score and Best Original Song for "Into the West", shared with Fran Walsh and Annie Lennox), plus a Golden Globe for RotK.

### Cited Findings
- Shore: "The use of the themes and the leitmotifs were used for clarity in storytelling." — [Ludwig-Van Toronto interview, Dec 2021](https://www.ludwig-van.com/toronto/2021/12/17/feature-composer-howard-shore-looks-back-on-his-work-for-the-lord-of-the-rings/)
- Shore: "I particularly liked the Sam/Frodo relationship, that's really at the heart of the story." He first read the books in the late 1960s, while on tour. — [Ludwig-Van](https://www.ludwig-van.com/toronto/2021/12/17/feature-composer-howard-shore-looks-back-on-his-work-for-the-lord-of-the-rings/)
- Shore, on History of the Ring and Frodo touching the Ring (quoted above); "Lothlórien is more exotic." — [FotR Annotated Score](https://www.elvish.org/gwaith/pdf/fotr_annotated_score_2.pdf)
- Awards: FotR won Best Original Score at the 74th Academy Awards. RotK won Best Original Score, the Golden Globe for Best Original Score, and Best Original Song ("Into the West": Fran Walsh, Howard Shore, Annie Lennox). — [Wikipedia: RotK soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Return_of_the_King_(soundtrack)); [Wikipedia: Into the West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Into_the_West_(song)); [Wikipedia: awards list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Howard_Shore)
- Critical view: RotK "has far less in common with its two predecessors than they had with each other". — [Filmtracks: RotK](https://www.filmtracks.com/titles/lord_rings03.html)
- An academic paper exists: "The Exposition of Themes in The Fellowship of the Ring: How Howard Shore Uses Leitmotif to Establish a Narrative". — [academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/39731168/The_Exposition_of_Themes_in_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring_How_Howard_Shore_Uses_Leitmotif_to_Establish_a_Narrative) (not read)

### Inferences
- Shore's stated aim, "clarity in storytelling", fits a puzzle game well. Leitmotifs can serve as **information channels** (who is present, what is relevant), not just emotional colour.
- *Unverified background recollection:* TTT's score was ruled ineligible for the Oscar because it reused FotR themes. Verify before citing.

- Other Shore interviews (e.g. in Adams' book, DVD appendices, Shore's Middle-earth symphony notes) could not be accessed.
- The TTT Oscar-eligibility issue is unverified.
