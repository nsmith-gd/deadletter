## 1. Who is Phil Lober? (background, credits, labels)

### Takeaway
Phil (Phillip) Lober is a Los Angeles-based composer of film, trailer and game music. He started very young: he scored a feature documentary as a teenager and won a festival score award for it at about 18. He is best known commercially for trailer-library cues, notably "Sidewinder" for Ghostwriter Music, used in the *Aquaman* final trailer. He also releases solo albums under his own name, in which he deliberately mixes epic-orchestral writing with genres the epic-music scene rarely uses, such as VOCALOID, cumbia, folk-country and neo-soul.

## 2. What is "If"? (identification, concept, track list, arc)

### Takeaway
"If" is not a film or game soundtrack. It is Phil Lober's debut solo studio album, released 31 March 2016: 11 tracks, about 51 minutes, distributed under his own artist name. Apple Music files it as Electronic, and Last.fm tags it "electronic, epic, cinematic". Its best-known track, "Alive", is an orchestral/epic song sung by the English VOCALOID voicebank AVANNA. It was his first piece using VOCALOID and was first published as a single in December 2015. No concept statement or liner notes were found.

### Disambiguation

No film, game or trailer titled "IF" is credited to him on IMDb or in any other source I found. Other "Phil/Philip" composers in the results (Phil Leuze, Philip Lasser, a Kraft-Engel composer with a *What/If* TV credit) are different people. — [IMDb](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm4176770/); [Spotify album](https://open.spotify.com/album/7n5BDr1bzibmIOHnSb4Yxe)
- Conclusion: the user's "soundtrack IF" is almost certainly the 2016 album *If*. Listeners often call cinematic/epic albums "soundtracks", and YouTube epic-music channels present the tracks as standalone "epic music", not as music for any project.

## 3. Instrumentation and production

### Takeaway
Verified: his trailer work is "hybrid" (orchestra plus electronics/sound design). *If* combines orchestral/epic writing with electronic production and a synthetic VOCALOID lead voice. Featured live soloists appear on other singles: Kelly Ryu (violin) on "Final Light", and a female vocal on "Selerium". He stresses production quality as make-or-break. I found no source on sample libraries versus live recording for *If*.

### Cited Findings
- "Sidewinder" is described as an "epic and powerful hybrid cue". — [Trackin' Trailer](https://trackintrailer.blogspot.com/2018/11/aquaman-final-trailer-song-music.html)
- *If* is filed as Electronic and tagged "electronic, epic, cinematic". — [Apple Music](https://music.apple.com/us/album/if/1099348612); [Last.fm](https://www.last.fm/music/Phil+Lober/_/Alive)
- "Alive" uses VOCALOID (the AVANNA voicebank) as lead vocal over an orchestral production. Mixing was by Generdyn. — [Vocaloid Wiki](https://vocaloid.fandom.com/wiki/Alive/Phillip_Lober)
- Other singles have featured performers and female vocals: "Final Light (ft. Kelly Ryu) [Emotional Uplifting Violin Score]" and "Selerium [Epic Music - Beautiful Female Vocal Orchestral]". — [YouTube Final Light](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F1VbbucGbww); [YouTube Selerium](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Dhlsc8teh1g)

- He calls himself a sound designer as well as a composer. — [Canvas Rebel](https://canvasrebel.com/meet-phil-lober/)

### Inferences
- Using a synthetic singer in place of a live choir or vocalist is the album's signature timbre. For a game, an artificial or "not-quite-human" voice over an organic orchestra is a strong way to suggest machines, ghosts, letters from the dead, or memory. It may fit a project titled "Dead Letter".
- The early backlash against VOCALOID suggests the voice sits prominently in the mix rather than buried. This is inference.

## 5. Form and dynamics

### Takeaway
Lober's own statements describe trailer form as compressed emotional narrative: a whole plot in a short summary, which he spent about a year learning ("structure and tonality"). *If* has trailer-length cues (2:46–4:05) alongside long-form 6-minute pieces ("Alive", "Divide", "Hokkaido"). This points to longer builds than trailer format allows, but no source describes the internal structure.

### Cited Findings
- Trailer music packages "an entire emotional plot into a short summary through music and editing". Learning "its structure and tonality" took about a year. — [Voyage LA](https://voyagela.com/interview/life-work-with-phil-lober)
- Tip: study a successful track's structure and atmosphere, borrow without copying, add personal style, and watch trends, because what editors want shifts. — [Evenant](https://www.evenant.com/articles/5-trailer-music-tips-from-phil-lober)
- Track lengths range from 1:26 (intro) to 6:17. — [Deezer](https://www.deezer.com/us/album/12777474)

### Inferences
- The 6-minute tracks probably use the standard epic-music arc (quiet opening → build → peak → coda), extended beyond the usual 2–3 minute trailer cue. This needs listening to confirm.
- For game use: his habit of writing "a plot in miniature" suits cutscenes and chapter-end stingers. The album's mix of short and long cues mirrors a game's split between short cues and extended exploration loops.

## 6. Emotional storytelling

### Takeaway
How the music is received and marketed is clear: "epic, dramatic, emotional, uplifting, powerful, beautiful". How it achieves that is not documented. The most instructive verified story is the "Alive" reception arc. Listeners first rejected the synthetic VOCALOID voice, then accepted it. Lober drew a philosophy from this: music should not be boxed by labels.

### Cited Findings
- Early reception of "Alive" was negative because of its VOCALOID use, but listeners "later came around". Lober concluded that "music, and life in general, can't fall under labels and be 100% accurate". — [Voyage LA, "Daily Inspiration"](https://voyagela.com/interview/daily-inspiration-meet-phil-lober/)
- Epic-music channels frame his work as "Epic Powerful", "Most Epic Dramatic Emotional & Uplifting", "Powerful & Beautiful Orchestral" and "Emotional Uplifting Violin Score". — [YouTube Alive](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kwAdiEv89eE); [Best Of mix](https://music.ava360.com/alive-best-of-phil-lober-1-hour-of-most-epic-dramatic-emotional-uplifting-music-mix_0c5a54148.html); [YouTube Music Best Of](https://music.youtube.com/watch?v=Ti50KjZv-i8&list=PLFIJZu91LCEkb_3GNIjB0yQyIL2VoQwZI)
- A fan tie-in to *Avatar: The Last Airbender* circulates with "Alive", which suggests listeners hear it as heroic and transformative. This is reported only in the search summary. — [Vocaloid Wiki / search context](https://vocaloid.fandom.com/wiki/Alive/Phillip_Lober)

### Inferences
- Track titles cover hope and agency ("Alive", "Stand to Light", "Fly", "We Rule the World"), conflict ("Divide"), childhood and family memory ("Mother", "Toybox") and loss ("Flowers Fall"). Even the loss-titled track is reported in a major key at a fast tempo, which suggests a bittersweet or "triumphant grief" treatment rather than a minor-key lament. Worth checking by ear.
- Lessons for a different game's score: (1) an unconventional lead timbre (a synthetic voice) can become the emotional signature, even if it divides listeners at first. (2) Mixing genres outside the epic-music canon (cumbia, folk-country, neo-soul, in his words) is a deliberate route to a distinctive voice.
