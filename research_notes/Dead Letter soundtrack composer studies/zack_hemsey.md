## 1. Background, working method, and notable placements

### Takeaway
Zack Hemsey (b. 1983) is a self-taught, independent, one-man-studio composer/songwriter/rapper. He writes, produces and mixes "in the box" (Logic Pro, Genelec monitors, sample libraries) and releases on his own label entity, Trees Of Earth LLC. His fame comes from "Mind Heist", the music in Inception's third trailer (2010), and "Vengeance" (The Equalizer, 2014, plus several trailers and promos). His albums mix alternative hip-hop, rock, orchestral and world elements rather than being pure trailer music.

### Inferences
- His sync success comes from licensing *pre-existing* album tracks ("Vengeance", "The Way", *Ronin* cues) rather than writing to picture on commission. "Mind Heist" is the main exception, made specifically for the trailer. This suggests his tracks are built as self-contained, editable dramatic arcs that editors can cut down.
- His single-person, in-the-box workflow with sample libraries "used unrealistically" is directly relevant to a small game team. The Hemsey sound is achievable without a live orchestra, and part of its character comes from *not* hiding that it is sampled.

## 2. Instrumentation and production

### Takeaway
Documented elements: sample-library orchestra (strings, brass) played aggressively and "unrealistically" up front; risers and synth effects under the strings; the brass "BRAAAM" as a musical (not just sound-design) gesture; and his own rapped and sung vocals over orchestral and rock beds on the albums. Detailed descriptions of specific percussion, guitar or piano voicings were not available in what I could reach.

### Cited Findings
- "Mind Heist": listeners note a lot going on underneath the strings, with "risers" and synth effects when the main theme arrives. The samples are used "in a very rhythmical/upfront unrealistic way", and the dynamics "build up very ingeniously". — [VI-Control thread](https://vi-control.net/community/threads/mind-heist-zack-hemsey.32920/) (forum opinion)
- BRAAAM lineage: Mike Zarin's first Inception teaser treated the low horn blast as sound design, built from subway and foley recordings. Zarin says Hemsey used that as a starting point and gave his version "a musical quality". Hemsey's version is the BRAAAM most audiences know, released as "Mind Heist". — [Collider](https://collider.com/inception-trailer-musical-cue/); [Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/braaams-beginners-how-a-horn-793220/); [Wikipedia – BRAAAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BRAAAM)
- Zimmer's own account of the film's BRAAAM, for comparison: brass played into a piano's resonance (sustain pedal held) in a church, plus "a bit of electronic nonsense". — [Wikipedia – BRAAAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BRAAAM) (via search summary, citing Vulture)
- Hemsey's blog post "BRAAAM 101" (Nov 2013) argues the device was *popularized* by "Mind Heist" and the Inception trailers but not invented there. — [Hemsey blog](http://zackhemsey.blogspot.com/2013/11/braaam-101.html) (via search summary; full text not read). Other histories trace early BRAAAM-like hits to the 2007 *Transformers* trailer. — [Epic Stock Media](https://epicstockmedia.com/a-brief-history-of-braaam/); [Northwestern "the origins of braaam"](https://sites.northwestern.edu/hundredthmile/the-origins-of-braaam)
- Vocals: *Ronin* is a rap album over soundtrack-style production. One RYM user says the production is strong and the lyrics come through clearly, but many tracks are repetitive and run about 6 minutes. Another credits him with blending soundtrack and rap styles. An AOTY user calls it "balanced by the most adrenaline-laced beats in Hemsey's catalog". — [RYM](https://rateyourmusic.com/release/album/zack-hemsey/ronin/); [AOTY](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/album/180183-zack-hemsey-ronin.php) (user reviews, not critics)
- Releasing instrumental editions (*Ronin (Instrumentals)*, *Nomad (Instrumental)*) and alternate versions ("Waiting Between Worlds" lyrical mix and instrumental; five "Mind Heist" versions). — [Bandcamp](https://zackhemsey.bandcamp.com/album/ronin-instrumentals); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zack_Hemsey); [filmdetail](https://filmdetail.com/2011/05/16/new-versions-of-mind-heist-zack-hemsey-inception/)
- Influences he reportedly named: the film scores *Last of the Mohicans*, *Conan the Barbarian*, *Crimson Tide* and *Braveheart*, and the rock bands Pearl Jam and Tool. — [FSM board, Zack Hemsey Interview, July 2011](https://filmscoremonthly.com/board/posts.cfm?threadID=80559) (attribution in the thread is not fully clear; secondhand)

### Inferences
- The influences line up with the sound. Mohicans, Crimson Tide and Braveheart supply heroic, modal, low-string and brass gravitas. Tool supplies long, repetitive, slowly intensifying structures with heavy low end. Pearl Jam supplies the earnest rock vocal. This is an inference from the influence list, not something Hemsey said.
- The alternate-mix habit (vocal and instrumental, short and "Evolution" versions) works like a *stem and intensity-layer* mindset, which carries straight over to vertical layering in adaptive game audio.
- No source I reached describes specific distorted-guitar, taiko or piano parts in named tracks. Any such claims in the final report should be labelled as listening impressions, not documentation.

### Gaps
- No written breakdown of percussion palette, guitar use, drone construction or vocal processing for specific tracks was reachable.
- Transcription leads exist but were not read: a [Scribd "Mind Heist" transcription](https://www.scribd.com/document/355521976/Zack-Hemsey-Mind-Heist) (about ♩=86).

## 3. Form: the trailer arc, pacing tension and release, ostinato and repetition

### Takeaway
Hemsey's own statement is that trailer tracks are usually *edited down* from longer works. The Inception version of "Mind Heist" is an abridged cut of a bigger piece, and "Mind Heist (Evolution)" expands it to about 6 minutes, building toward a massive climax. Listeners describe his writing as built on repetition and long gradual builds. A detailed bar-by-bar structural analysis was not found.

### Cited Findings
- In "BRAAAM 101" Hemsey explains that most songs exceed the roughly 2-minute length of a theatrical trailer, so trailer songs are usually edited. The Inception version is an abridged version of a larger work. — [Hemsey blog](http://zackhemsey.blogspot.com/2013/11/braaam-101.html) (via search summary)
- An earlier AdTunes interview (now lost) has Hemsey describing a constant back-and-forth between the edit and the music on trailer work. — [Trailer Music News](https://trailermusicnews.com/?p=8718) (secondhand recollection; the original could not be found by that author)
- "Mind Heist (Evolution)" expands the original to about 6 minutes and "builds towards a massive sounding climax". — [filmdetail](https://filmdetail.com/2011/05/16/new-versions-of-mind-heist-zack-hemsey-inception/) (via search summary)
- Listener criticism of *Ronin* says many tracks are repetitive and run around six minutes. The positive reading is a long-form ostinato approach; the negative is that it can lose attention. — [RYM](https://rateyourmusic.com/release/album/zack-hemsey/ronin/)
- "Mind Heist" dynamics "build up very ingeniously", with risers and synth FX signalling the arrival of the main theme. — [VI-Control](https://vi-control.net/community/threads/mind-heist-zack-hemsey.32920/)
- Grokipedia characterises his trailer work as "orchestral compositions which blended epic builds with intense rhythmic elements". **[weak source]** — [Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/Zack_Hemsey)
- Derek Lieu, a game trailer editor, wrote a piece on the Inception BRAAAM sound for game-trailer practice. Its contents were not read. — [Derek Lieu blog](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2017/12/3/the-inception-braaaaaaam-sound)

### Inferences
- His tracks appear to be written as long, self-contained arcs (about 4–6 minutes) that editors cut to about 2 minutes. That makes them structurally different from purpose-built trailer cues with fixed hit points. Pacing seems to come from adding layers over a repeating figure (risers, then the theme entering, then the full climax) rather than from frequent changes of material.
- For game scoring, this "long arc made of stackable layers over one ostinato" design maps naturally onto vertical remixing. The "abridged for trailer" practice maps onto horizontal re-sequencing, where you cut between intensity sections of one master track.

### Gaps
- No timing map of "Mind Heist", "Vengeance" or "The Way" (sparse intro, build, climax, sting) was available in written form.

## 4. Harmonic language

### Takeaway
I found almost no reliable written harmonic analysis of Hemsey's music. The only data points are crowd-sourced key and tempo estimates for "Mind Heist", and they contradict each other.

### Cited Findings
- "Mind Heist" key and tempo conflict: Tunebat says A♭ major at 92 BPM; Chordify says C minor at 188 BPM (likely double-time); a sheet transcription gives ♩≈86. — [Tunebat](https://tunebat.com/Info/Mind-Heist-Zack-Hemsey/3P5OnuqwVhZMEMoaZa9GuE); [Chordify](https://chordify.net/chords/zack-hemsey-songs/mind-heist-chords); [Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/355521976/Zack-Hemsey-Mind-Heist)
- Note: A♭ major and C minor share most of their pitches (C minor's relative major is E♭, but A♭ major and C minor differ by one note). The disagreement fits a tonally ambiguous piece built on a modal minor centre, but this is my interpretation. — inference from [Tunebat](https://tunebat.com/Info/Mind-Heist-Zack-Hemsey/3P5OnuqwVhZMEMoaZa9GuE) and [Chordify](https://chordify.net/chords/zack-hemsey-songs/mind-heist-chords)
- Hemsey's 2011 blog post about Steve Jablonsky's *Transformers: Dark of the Moon* cue "It's Our Fight" addresses claimed similarity between that cue and his work. He draws a line between influence and plagiarism. Fans separately complained that a TF3 cue "rips off Inception". — [Hemsey blog](http://zackhemsey.blogspot.com/2011/07/its-our-fight-bysteve-jablonsky.html); [TFW2005 forum](https://www.tfw2005.com/boards/threads/theres-a-song-in-the-movie-that-blatantly-rips-off-inception.425991/) (the exact direction of the accusation was ambiguous in the search summary)

### Inferences (unverified; based on general trailer-genre conventions, not documented Hemsey analysis)
- Expect minor-key ostinatos over a held bass or pedal, with modal colour (Aeolian, Dorian or Phrygian inflections) and slow-moving submediant motion (i–VI–III–VII-type loops), which was standard in the 2010-era Zimmer-adjacent trailer idiom. **Do not present these as documented Hemsey progressions.**
- The tonal ambiguity hinted at by the conflicting key reads (A♭ major vs C minor) would be one way he gets "gravitas without resolution". The report should frame this as a hypothesis.

### Gaps
- No analysis of chord progressions for "Vengeance", "The Way", "Redemption" or "See What I've Become" was found. A composer studying this will need ear transcription or the Scribd transcription.

## 5. Emotional register: dread, resolve, gravitas, melancholy, weight and scale

### Takeaway
The documented register combines epic weight (the BRAAAM as a musical event, massive climaxes) with introspective, identity-focused lyrical themes. The Viral Hare interview frames his work as going beyond trailer music to touch on identity and "life's journey", and *Goliath* is a concept score about ambition and downfall.

### Cited Findings
- Viral Hare interview framing: his work goes beyond trailer music, touching on identity and life's journey. — [Viral Hare](https://viralhare.com/a-conversation-with-composer-zack-hemsey-featured-in-inception-equalizer/)
- *Goliath* is described as an original soundtrack for a fictional film that tells a story of ambition and downfall. **[weak source]** — [Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/Zack_Hemsey)
- Song and album titles show the thematic territory: "Vengeance", "Redemption", "See What I've Become", "The Way", *Ronin* ("Teachings Of A Ronin"), *Nomad*, *Goliath*, "Waiting Between Worlds", "Empty Room". — [Shazam](https://www.shazam.com/song/1649221468/redemption); [Bandcamp collection](https://bandcamp.com/drakain); [Discogs](https://www.discogs.com/release/11717101-Zack-Hemsey-Goliath-Original-Motion-Picture-Soundtrack-To-A-Film-That-Doesnt-Exist)
- Sports gravitas: "Vengeance" was chosen for *The Last Dance* (Michael Jordan documentary), and "Mind Heist" opened *Madden NFL 12*. — [Tunefind](https://www.tunefind.com/song/zack-hemsey/vengeance); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zack_Hemsey)

### Inferences
- The titles point to a recurring arc of transformation, loss, retribution and redemption, often in the first person ("See What I've Become"). That suits a protagonist-centred narrative game where the score tracks the character's inner state, not just the stakes on screen.
- Weight and scale seem to come from three things: (a) low brass and synth blasts treated as *musical* events (the BRAAAM), (b) a long build over one ostinato so that arrival feels earned, and (c) hyper-real, upfront sample mixing rather than a concert-hall perspective. Points (a) and (c) are sourced to the descriptions above; how they combine is my inference.

### Gaps
- No written description of the specific emotional turn inside "Vengeance" or "The Way" (for example, where a track shifts from dread to resolve).

## 6. Transferability: what carries over to narrative game scoring vs what is trailer-specific

### Takeaway
What carries over: long-form ostinato builds, layered intensity versions (vocal, instrumental, extended), the concept-album "score for a film that doesn't exist" approach, and identity-driven thematic writing. What is trailer-specific: front-loaded BRAAAM hits, a compressed 2-minute arc, and climax-then-sting endings, which tire listeners quickly over hours of gameplay.

### Cited Findings
- His "Mind Heist" was repurposed as the intro video for *Madden NFL 12*, which shows his trailer idiom transfers to game *cinematics*. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zack_Hemsey)
- **[weak source]** "The Way" in the *Transformers: Fall of Cybertron* launch trailer. — [Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/Zack_Hemsey)
- On *Goliath*, Hemsey said writing a soundtrack for a non-existent film is "no different than any other album": a confluence of circumstances that leads him to have something to say creatively. He pursues whatever speaks to him creatively regardless of practical or commercial use, and he would not sign up to score a future film he knows nothing about. — [Viral Hare](https://viralhare.com/a-conversation-with-composer-zack-hemsey-featured-in-inception-equalizer/) (via search summary, close paraphrase)
- He says he doesn't chase inspiration; he composes when inspired. — [Viral Hare](https://viralhare.com/a-conversation-with-composer-zack-hemsey-featured-in-inception-equalizer/) (via search summary)
- Zimmer has publicly said he dislikes the Inception BRAAAM being overused in trailers, which shows the gesture goes stale through repetition. — [Dark Horizons](https://www.darkhorizons.com/zimmer-hates-inception-braaam-in-trailers/)

### Inferences
- **Carries over well:**
  - One-ostinato, many-layers design: write the full 5–6 minute arc as one track, then export intensity tiers (as with "Mind Heist" and its "Evolution" version) for vertical mixing in exploration, tension and combat states.
  - Instrumental and vocal twins (*Ronin* / *Ronin (Instrumentals)*): use a sung or spoken-word version for key story beats or credits, and the instrumental for gameplay loops.
  - Concept-score approach (*Goliath*): writing a "score for an unmade film" is essentially how a game composer drafts a theme suite before levels exist.
  - Identity and transformation themes ("See What I've Become", "Redemption", "Vengeance"): suited to leitmotifs tied to a character's arc.
  - One-person, in-the-box, hyper-real sample aesthetic: feasible for an indie game budget and part of the style rather than a compromise.
- **Trailer-specific (use sparingly in a game):**
  - The BRAAAM. It now reads as a 2010s trailer cliché (see Zimmer's comment), so save it for a single signature reveal or boss moment, if you use it at all.
  - Climax-then-hard-stop "sting" endings and riser-into-hit transitions. In gameplay these call for state-change triggers, not loops.
  - Repetitiveness. RYM listeners already find six-minute repetitive tracks tiring on an album. In a looping game context, the ostinato needs variation layers or alternate takes.

### Gaps
- I found no statements from Hemsey about games or interactive music specifically.

## 7. Composer's own statements and reception

### Takeaway
Hemsey's public voice is mostly his blog ("thoughts & ramblings") and a few interviews. In them he corrects misattribution ("I am not Hans Zimmer"), explains trailer editing and BRAAAM history, separates influence from plagiarism, and stresses creative independence. Critical reception is thin: no professional album reviews were found, user ratings are mid-range, and his popular reach comes mainly through sync placements.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- He has a clear artistic identity separate from the trailer industry: a rapper and songwriter who writes cinematic beds, who resists being filed as a Zimmer imitator, and who releases on his own terms. For a game composer, the lesson is that a recognisable personal voice (vocals, lyrical themes) can sit on top of a standard epic-hybrid toolkit.
