## 1. Instrumentation, production and the "campfire" folk aesthetic

### Takeaway
Prahlow built the Hearthian side of the score on a deliberately humble, "unexpected-in-space" palette: an old gift banjo, acoustic guitar and harmonica. He set this against ambient, post-rock-influenced synth textures for the alien (Nomai) side, all in service of "simplicity and nostalgia". He composed, performed and recorded the music himself, a one-person, home-studio approach. For Echoes of the Eye he kept the method but darkened the palette, using a Prophet-6 and a Moog-plus-slide-guitar hybrid.

### Cited Findings
- **Origin of the banjo sound.** Prahlow traced the main theme's banjo to "an old beat up banjo that I had received as a gift a few years prior". He said he aimed for "a sense of simplicity and nostalgia as the player slowly becomes familiar with the world of the Hearthians". — [Film Music Central interview, 2020](https://filmmusiccentral.com/2020/03/26/an-interview-with-andrew-prahlow-composer-of-outer-wilds/) (as reported in search extract; also echoed in [USC Thornton](https://music.usc.edu/andrew-prahlow-earns-two-award-nominations-for-outer-wilds-soundtrack/))
- **Two sound-worlds.** Prahlow contrasts the folk/banjo material for the Hearthians with ambient, post-rock-influenced textures for the Nomai. — [Film Music Central](https://filmmusiccentral.com/2020/03/26/an-interview-with-andrew-prahlow-composer-of-outer-wilds/) (search extract); early concepts contrasted "the Hearthians' folk instruments with the Nomai's style" — [Game Developer 2019](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/behind-the-hauntingly-beautiful-music-of-i-outer-wilds-i-) (search extract)
- **Unexpected-in-space timbres.** The score uses "sounds that players wouldn't expect to hear in a game set in outer space, such as a plucky banjo and harmonica". — [USC Thornton](https://music.usc.edu/andrew-prahlow-earns-two-award-nominations-for-outer-wilds-soundtrack/) (search extract)
- **Stylistic references.** Prahlow said he "was able to use a lot of his post-rock and indie-rock references from the late '90s and early 2000s". — [SPIN, June 2020](https://www.spinmagazine.com/2020/06/outer-wilds-andrew-prahlow/) (search extract). Spectrum Culture describes the score as one that "meanders through folk, post-rock, ambient, electronic and cinematic influences". — [Spectrum Culture, 2026](https://spectrumculture.com/2026/10/20/anatomy-of-a-tracklist-andrew-prahlow-outer-wilds-original-soundtrack/)
- **Banjo dominance and synths.** A review says the banjo dominates much of the score alongside synths and electronic textures, and that "the score becomes more complex the farther along the story you go". — [Film Music Central soundtrack review, 2020](https://filmmusiccentral.com/2020/03/19/soundtrack-review-outer-wilds-2019/) (search extract)
- **"Nostalgia for the unknown."** The rustic acoustic palette (banjo, harmonica) grounds the sci-fi setting, framed as "nostalgia for the unknown". — [Shapes Inc. fan guide](https://shapes.inc/fandom/outer-wilds/author). This is a low-reliability fan aggregator, and I could not find the phrase attributed to Prahlow in a primary source.
- **Self-performed and self-recorded.** The Lost Reels is credited as "all music composed, performed, and recorded by Andrew Prahlow". — [Bandcamp, The Lost Reels](https://andrewprahlow.bandcamp.com/album/outer-wilds-echoes-of-the-eye-the-lost-reels-deluxe-original-game-soundtrack-2) (via search extract)
- **Synths across both games.** Prahlow used a Moog on both games. On Echoes of the Eye he "became captivated by the Prophet 6". — [Game Developer 2022](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/everything-but-lost-exploring-the-mesmerising-musical-legacy-of-outer-wilds) (search extract)
- **The "theremin" that isn't one.** The new traveler instrument in Echoes that people take for a theremin "is actually a Moog he mixed with a haunted slide guitar he found in New Orleans" (on a 2019 trip). — [Game Developer 2022](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/everything-but-lost-exploring-the-mesmerising-musical-legacy-of-outer-wilds) (search extract)
- **His instrument collection.** Prahlow appeared on a Rolling Stone livestream (Nov 2022) about Outer Wilds and "his instrument collection". — [Andrew Prahlow on X](https://x.com/andrewprahlow/status/1594829894210707457); [Twitch VOD](https://www.twitch.tv/videos/1659357885)
- **Long development.** The project began around 2012 and shipped in 2019. Prahlow was brought on by his friend Alex Beachum. — [Film Music Central](https://filmmusiccentral.com/2020/03/26/an-interview-with-andrew-prahlow-composer-of-outer-wilds/) (search extract). The game began as Beachum's Master's thesis at USC, where Prahlow met the team. — [Spectrum Culture](https://spectrumculture.com/2026/10/20/anatomy-of-a-tracklist-andrew-prahlow-outer-wilds-original-soundtrack/); [Skewed 'n Reviewed, 2020](https://www.sknr.net/2020/03/30/andrew-prahlow-talks-composing-outer-wilds-and-the-pending-live-show/)
- **Early, close collaboration.** Prahlow worked "in close proximity with Mobius from the outset so the score would be central to the experience". — [Game Developer 2022](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/everything-but-lost-exploring-the-mesmerising-musical-legacy-of-outer-wilds) (search extract)

### Inferences
- The production lesson is **timbral authenticity over scale**. A cheap, worn, self-played instrument (a gift banjo, harmonica) reads as "people", "home" and "campfire". Against the vastness of space, that intimacy is what produces the emotion. A composer can borrow the principle: one organic, slightly imperfect "home" instrument family, with alien or ambient synth worlds set against it.
- The two-palette split (folk = the living Hearthians and the player; ambient/post-rock synths = the dead Nomai and cosmic mystery) works as an **instrumentation-as-faction** system. It lets the player know *whose story* they are in by timbre alone.
- The Prophet-6 and Moog-plus-slide hybrid in Echoes shows how to extend an established palette for a DLC or second act. Keep the "human-played" gesture (slide guitar), but bend it through analogue synthesis until it sounds uncanny.

### Gaps
- No source available here detailed mic technique, rooms, mixing or DAW. Interviews describe a home/self-recorded approach but not specifics.
- Whether acoustic guitar, strings or piano feature prominently, and on which tracks, could not be confirmed from readable sources. A "piano cue" is mentioned once (see §4). Strings appear in the later live arrangements: [Hollywood Chamber Orchestra](https://hollywoodchamberorchestra.com/?p=485).

## 2. Diegetic music: the Travelers' instruments, the Signalscope and the final union ("Travelers")

### Takeaway
Each of the five Hearthian Travelers plays one part of a **single shared song** on a different instrument, each on a different planet. The player can tune into each part from anywhere in the solar system with the Signalscope, a radio-telescope-like tool. At the Eye of the Universe, the player gathers the Travelers around a campfire and the parts combine into the full ensemble piece "Travelers". Optional characters (Solanum, and in Echoes the Prisoner) can add further voices. Prahlow says the idea of one song split across the band existed from the start and was planned with Beachum.

### Cited Findings
- **One song, split across a band.** Prahlow said the original idea was that the Travelers would play one song together, like parts of a band, rather than each playing a separate tune. The game reveals this gradually. — [SPIN](https://www.spinmagazine.com/2020/06/outer-wilds-andrew-prahlow/) (as summarised in search extract; a second search on the same article did not resurface the phrase, so treat the exact wording as unconfirmed)
- **Planned with Beachum from the start.** Prahlow and director Alex Beachum talked early on about how the Travelers and their instruments could work inside the game. — [SPIN](https://www.spinmagazine.com/2020/06/outer-wilds-andrew-prahlow/) (search extract)
- **Instruments chosen from Beachum's character sketches.** Prahlow picked instruments to match each character's personality, working from Beachum's sketches. — [Game Developer 2022](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/everything-but-lost-exploring-the-mesmerising-musical-legacy-of-outer-wilds) (search extract)
- **Mechanic.** "Each planet has its own sound, and a member of the exploration program plays the same song on a different instrument." You can hear them from anywhere with the Signalscope. — [Wikipedia, Outer Wilds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_Wilds) (search extract). The Signalscope is described as a multi-frequency receiver that homes in on distant signals. — [Game Developer 2022](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/everything-but-lost-exploring-the-mesmerising-musical-legacy-of-outer-wilds)
- **Instrument-to-traveler-to-location map** (fan wiki/guide level):
  - Chert: drums, Ember Twin
  - Gabbro: flute, Giant's Deep
  - Riebeck: banjo, Brittle Hollow
  - Feldspar: harmonica, Dark Bramble (also audible on Timber Hearth)
  - Esker: whistling, Attlerock (Esker whistles rather than plays an instrument)

### Inferences
- **Implementation (inferred, not confirmed).** The described behaviour implies the following. Each traveler part is a tempo-locked stem looping in sync on a positional emitter. The Signalscope acts as a directional "listening" filter or attenuation control over those emitters. At the Eye, the stems are summed in sync, with optional extra stems added depending on who is present. I found **no developer source (GDC talk, postmortem, middleware name) documenting the music system**, so this is engineering inference.
- **The core storytelling technique is "distributed theme, delayed assembly".** The player hears fragments (one instrument, far away, through a crackly scope) for the whole game. They may never consciously register that the fragments are one piece. The finale assembles them, so the emotional payoff is *recognition* rather than novelty. For another game: scatter a theme's voices across locations or characters as diegetic sources, and pay off by reuniting them.
- **Music doubles as a navigation tool.** Signals are literal wayfinding. The theme is learned through play, so the finale feels earned.
- **Consent as a musical beat.** The Prisoner asks to join, and the player chooses. This turns adding one stem into a moral or emotional act of inclusion. That is a strong technique for redemption arcs: let the player decide whether a once-hostile or estranged voice joins the ensemble.
- **Timbral contrast at the payoff.** The Prisoner's uncanny synth-slide voice joining the folk ensemble resolves the Echoes horror palette *into* the home palette. The estranged sound is reconciled harmonically and socially.

### Gaps
- No primary Mobius source (Beachum, Loan Verneau or audio programmers) on how the Signalscope and stem sync were built. No GDC talk on music implementation surfaced in searches.
- The exact key, tempo and part-writing of "Travelers" could not be confirmed. A fan forum claims the "Travelers" theme is in G major ([Young Composers forum](https://www.youngcomposers.com/t44920/sonata-on-the-themes-of-outer-wilds/)), which is unverified amateur analysis.
- What precisely Solanum contributes (vocal? Nomai instrument?) was not confirmed by a readable source.

## 4. Music and the time loop: "End Times" as signal and timer

### Takeaway
"End Times" is the score's single most important functional cue. It plays in the last ~2 minutes of the 22-minute loop, audible anywhere in the system, overriding other music, and the sun goes supernova as it ends. It is both a diegetic-feeling alarm and a non-diegetic emotional coda. Prahlow confirms its purpose is to warn of the reset.

### Cited Findings
- **Timing.** "In the last 2 minutes before the Sun goes supernova, the song End Times plays, which can be heard from any location. Upon the song finishing, the Sun goes supernova." — [Outer Wilds Wiki, Supernova](https://outerwilds.fandom.com/wiki/Supernova) (search extract). The wiki lists the track at 1:58. — [Outer Wilds Wiki OST](https://outerwilds.fandom.com/wiki/Outer_Wilds_OST)
- **Conflicting duration.** TV Tropes says it starts about a minute and a half before the explosion, "replac[ing] whatever music is currently playing", and gives "a chilling warning to players that they'd better wrap up what they're doing". — [TV Tropes, Outer Wilds](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/OuterWilds). This conflicts slightly with the wiki's 2 minutes.
- **Composer intent.** Prahlow told Prima Games that End Times begins near the end of each loop to warn of the reset. A piano cue nudges stuck players toward key ruins. — as reported in search extract citing [Prima Games](https://primagames.com/featured/developing-music-outer-wilds-interview-composer-andrew-prahlow). This came via an unnamed secondary write-up; the "piano cue" detail especially needs checking in the original.
- **The mechanical analogy.** Epilogue Gaming compares End Times to the Super Mario Bros. "running out of time" theme, as "one of the game's many auditory systems to aid its gameplay". — [Epilogue Gaming](https://epiloguegaming.com/a-universe-of-wonder/)
- **How it creates urgency.** A reviewer says the rushed feeling comes from fluctuating the melody's volume, paralleling the Main Title alarms. They also call it climactic but part of "the streak of quiet ambient tracks". — [AOTY user review](https://www.albumoftheyear.org/user/oakred/album/221499-outer-wilds-original-soundtrack/)
- **Player experience.** A ResetEra user notes "an undercurrent of something *else*" in the track, and that it "keeps going beyond when the supernova would be…". — [ResetEra RTTP thread](https://www.resetera.com/threads/rttp-outer-wilds-soundtrack.771614/)
- **Lost Reels callback.** A Lost Reels suite is titled "River's End Times", combining the Echoes "River's End" with "End Times". — [Khinsider](https://downloads.khinsider.com/game-soundtracks/album/outer-wilds-echoes-of-the-eye-the-lost-reels-deluxe-original-game-soundtrack-2022)

### Inferences
- **"Music as timer" without a HUD.** Outer Wilds has no visible loop clock in normal play. End Times is the *only* universal, location-independent timer, so the player learns to read music as time. After a few loops, its first notes trigger a learned emotional and Pavlovian response: resignation, urgency, or a decision to watch the sun. One cue thus carries mechanical information *and* the game's thematic acceptance of endings.
- **Ritual through repetition.** The same cue every loop, with a fixed relationship to an irreversible event, turns repetition into ritual rather than annoyance. That is unusual, because most game loops make players resent repeated music. It works partly because the rest of the score is sparse (§5), so End Times is not competing with wallpaper music.
- **The design lesson for a loop or deadline game.** Bind one dedicated cue to the deadline. Let it override everything. End the cue *on* the event, so the music's cadence *is* the catastrophe. Write it so it can be heard as both alarm and elegy.

## 5. Silence, ambience and sparseness

### Takeaway
Silence is policy, not absence. Prahlow states that constant looping music in an exploration game becomes "wallpaper". He treated Outer Wilds "like a film", with music entering only at emotionally important moments and sometimes carrying "clues". Most of the game has no music, which gives each cue weight.

### Cited Findings
- **The "wallpaper" principle.** "When there's constant looping music in an exploration game like Outer Wilds, the music can lose importance and become wallpaper… To avoid that and make the music stand out, I crafted the music to follow the player's sense of exploration." — [Game Developer 2019](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/behind-the-hauntingly-beautiful-music-of-i-outer-wilds-i-) (quoted in search extract)
- **Deliberate quiet.** "If you're just exploring under your own steam, admiring the stars, it'll be quiet. This was a deliberate choice by composer Andrew Prahlow." — [Game Developer 2019](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/behind-the-hauntingly-beautiful-music-of-i-outer-wilds-i-) (writer's paraphrase)
- **Film logic.** The approach was "making sure that music only played during emotionally important segments of gameplay". Prahlow: "The easiest way to avoid the 'wallpaper' type quality was treating Outer Wilds like a film"; in many of his favourite film scores, "the music isn't constant". — [Prima Games](https://primagames.com/featured/developing-music-outer-wilds-interview-composer-andrew-prahlow) (partly paraphrased by Prima)
- **Music as clues.** Prahlow focused on music that gives the player "clues". "There's no music playing for most of the game, so when the music enters it's really important." — [Film Music Central](https://filmmusiccentral.com/2020/03/26/an-interview-with-andrew-prahlow-composer-of-outer-wilds/) (search extract, partly paraphrased)
- **Suites into interactive loops.** His workflow was to write suites first, then take material from the suite, turn it into loops, and work out ways to make it interactive, with fades for natural transitions. — [Film Music Central](https://filmmusiccentral.com/2020/03/26/an-interview-with-andrew-prahlow-composer-of-outer-wilds/) (search extract)
- **The Eye's silence.** At the Eye, the campfire song "breaks through the cold, dark silence of this place". — [SVG](https://www.svg.com/385123/outer-wilds-ending-explained/) (search extract)

### Inferences
- The **suite-first, loop-second** workflow is a transferable method. Compose a through-composed piece with real arc (good for the album). Then mine it for interactive loops that fade in on discovery. This explains why the OST works as a listening album while the in-game use stays sparse.
- In the absence of score, the Travelers' diegetic signals and ambience become the "music" of exploration. So the game is never truly silent: it hands the musical role to diegetic sources. The sparse non-diegetic score keeps the diegetic campfire material emotionally clean for the finale.
- **"Clue" cues as reward signals.** Entering a key ruin or discovery triggers a cue (e.g., "The Search" at the Southern Observatory / Control Module; "Castaways" at the Interloper core). Music then works as *confirmation that you found something important*. That is a powerful discovery-reinforcement tool for investigation or mystery games.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data (e.g., percentage of playtime with score) was found.
- No ambience or sound-design credits or interviews (separate from Prahlow) surfaced.

## 6. Echoes of the Eye: how the score differs

### Takeaway
Prahlow kept his method but went "slightly darker". The DLC is described as introducing true horror and "a descent into madness". The palette shifts toward alien and hostile textures: echoing choral writing, staccato soundscapes, the Prophet-6, and the Moog plus haunted slide guitar that becomes the Prisoner's instrument. Its emotional payoff is the Prisoner's invited entry into the Travelers' campfire song.

### Cited Findings
- **Darker, same method.** Prahlow describes making Echoes "slightly darker in tone without changing his creative approach". — [Game Developer 2022](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/everything-but-lost-exploring-the-mesmerising-musical-legacy-of-outer-wilds) (search extract)
- **Horror and madness.** Coverage says the DLC added "something the Traveler was yet to experience, true horror". Prahlow calls the expansion "a little bit of a descent into madness". — [Game Developer 2022](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/everything-but-lost-exploring-the-mesmerising-musical-legacy-of-outer-wilds); [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/outer-wilds-echoes-of-the-eye-music-lost-reels-andrew-prahlow/); Bandcamp blurb on "a descent into madness" — [Bandcamp](https://andrewprahlow.bandcamp.com/album/outer-wilds-echoes-of-the-eye-the-lost-reels-deluxe-original-game-soundtrack-2) (search extracts)
- **Into uncharted territory.** Prahlow aimed to capture the move from familiar space into uncharted territory, tied to the Stranger, the cloaked station watching the system. — [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/outer-wilds-echoes-of-the-eye-music-lost-reels-andrew-prahlow/) (search extract)
- **Critical description.** The score is "a more alien and sometimes even more hostile take on the Outer Wilds audio universe, dipping into echoing choral arrangements and staccato soundscapes". — review text quoted in search extract from [Turntable Lab](https://www.turntablelab.com/products/andrew-prahlow-outer-wilds-echoes-of-the-eye-vinyl-lp) / [Black Screen Records](https://blackscreenrecords.com/products/outer-wilds-echoes-of-the-eye)
- **New instruments.** Prophet-6, plus the Moog/slide-guitar hybrid people take for a theremin. — [Game Developer 2022](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/everything-but-lost-exploring-the-mesmerising-musical-legacy-of-outer-wilds)
- **The opening's first sketch.** Prahlow posted a video from 25 June described as "the first recording of what became the opening to 'Echoes of the Eye'". — [Andrew Prahlow on X](https://x.com/andrewprahlow/status/1450982492677099523?lang=en)
- **The Prisoner's musical arc.** A theremin-like signal leads to the Prisoner's grave and instrument. At the Eye, the Prisoner asks permission to join and then plays. The encore adds "a customized synth instrument" as "one last voice". — [Outer Wilds Wiki, The Prisoner](https://outerwilds.fandom.com/wiki/Prisoner); [TV Tropes, Heartwarming](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Heartwarming/OuterWilds)
- **Track titles evoking the dream and the archive.** "A Dream of Home", "Dark Passage", "Sealed Away", "The Forbidden Archives", "The Lost Waltz", "Strange Flames", "Test Chamber Three", "River's End". — [VGMdb](https://vgmdb.net/album/115560); [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/outer-wilds-echoes-of-the-eye-original-soundtrack--mw0004416002)

### Inferences
- **A coherent DLC strategy.** Keep the human gesture (hand-played slide guitar) but process or blend it with analogue synths, so the new faction sounds like a corrupted mirror of the home folk palette rather than an unrelated genre. This lets the final reconciliation (Prisoner joins campfire) land musically.
- "The Lost Waltz" and "A Dream of Home" suggest the dream world uses nostalgic dance and home idioms rendered uncanny. That is a classic horror device, familiar forms made strange. This is inferred from titles only.

## 7. Prahlow's own statements, reception and awards

### Takeaway
The score was BAFTA-nominated (2020) and won or was nominated across major game-music awards. The Echoes score won the 2022 G.A.N.G. Award for Best Music for an Indie Game, and The Lost Reels was in contention for the first Grammy for Best Score Soundtrack for Video Games. Prahlow consistently frames the music around connection, nostalgia, and avoiding "wallpaper".

### Cited Findings
- **BAFTA 2020.** The Outer Wilds soundtrack was nominated for a 2020 BAFTA Games Award (Music); USC's page is dated 5 March 2020, ceremony 2 April. — [USC Thornton, BAFTA nomination](https://music.usc.edu/andrew-prahlow-nominated-for-bafta-games-award/)
